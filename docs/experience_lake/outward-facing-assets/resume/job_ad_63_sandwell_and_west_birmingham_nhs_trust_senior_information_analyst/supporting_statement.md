# Supporting Statement

I am applying for the Senior Information Analyst role because my experience aligns strongly with the post's focus on accurate, validated and meaningful information for performance monitoring, assurance, planning and decision-making. I understand this role supports Surgical Services and Women and Child Health, with emphasis on contracting activity, maternity data submissions, national targets, board assurance, regulator scrutiny, data quality and service improvement. I would bring operational reporting experience, advanced Excel and SQL capability, Power BI reporting, data-quality discipline, national-guidance interpretation, junior staff support and clear communication with technical and non-technical users.

I meet the formal analytical and numerate requirements through a First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter, completed with Distinction. My postgraduate work strengthened my ability to handle complex datasets, assess data quality, apply analytical methods and explain limitations clearly. I am confident working with formulas, percentages, variances, trends, graphs, validation checks and large reports of figures, and I am willing to complete a supervisory management qualification.

My strongest workplace evidence comes from my Data Analyst role at South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I supported recurring operational reports across service activity, equipment movement, QHSE, man-hours, close-out documentation and management information. I used Oracle data extracts as structured operational sources, SAS programs for repeatable analytical processing, Excel and Access for reporting inputs and checks, and Power BI for presenting trends, status views and exceptions. I produced reporting outputs showing which jobs were active, delayed, field-completed, awaiting documentation, awaiting equipment confirmation or ready for close-out.

That experience required me to validate complex reports and interpret ambiguous information across several data sources. A job could be marked as completed in one source but still be missing a signed service report, equipment return confirmation, man-hour record or close-out status. I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, inconsistent statuses, failed joins, unclear service categories and unexpected movements between reporting periods. That is the reporting assurance discipline I would bring to national returns, contracting activity, maternity submissions and performance monitoring: figures should not be published or commented on until the source, definition, transformation and exception position are understood.

To make my preparation for acute information reporting practical, I completed an independent reporting exercise using public NHS reporting publications and synthetic pathway records. This was not NHS employment or official submission ownership. I built a SQL Server reporting database, SQL Server Integration Services loading and validation process, SQL Server Reporting Services reports, a Power BI dashboard and an Excel validation pack covering waiting-list, contracting and maternity reporting concepts. The exercise helped me practise translating published definitions into local reporting rules, validation exceptions, commentary and handover notes, while keeping clear that final NHS reporting would require local PAS data, maternity system data, Trust sign-off processes and guidance from senior information colleagues.

I have strong experience with the report-writing and analysis tools named in the role. In Excel, I have built structured reporting workbooks, pivot tables, lookups, formulas, conditional formatting, validation checks, exception lists, graphs and management summaries, and used macros and VBA to automate repeated workbook checks, formatting and report-preparation steps. I have used SQL to create reporting queries, joins, validation checks and curated outputs for dashboards and analysis. I used SQL Server Reporting Services in the acute reporting project to produce paginated reports for waiting-list performance, contracting reconciliation, maternity data quality and exception review. I used SQL Server Integration Services to load, transform and validate reporting files into staging and reporting tables.

Data quality is central to how I work. At South Western, I learned not to treat missing submissions as true zero values. A service line with low recorded incidents could reflect low risk, but it could also reflect incomplete reporting. I used rates such as observations per 1,000 man-hours, trend views, standard deviation, median and mean comparisons, Pareto breakdowns and completeness checks to improve interpretation. In the acute reporting project, I applied the same thinking to waiting-list, contracting and maternity data by separating final from provisional figures, showing records requiring validation, reconciling activity across sources, and making clear whether movement appeared operational or data-quality related.

I am comfortable translating guidance into reporting logic. My approach is to identify the reporting question, define what is included and excluded, identify the authoritative source, map required fields, build validation checks, document assumptions and make uncertainty visible before a report is treated as final. At South Western, I applied this discipline by clarifying whether "completed" meant field-completed, documentation-complete or ready for close-out. In the acute reporting project, I practised converting referral-to-treatment and maternity reporting concepts into SQL rules, report filters, validation exceptions and commentary.

A key strength I would bring is using information to present a cogent argument. Managers do not need raw extracts; they need to know what changed, what is reliable, what needs action and what risks sit behind the figures. At South Western, separating "field-completed" from "ready for close-out" helped discussions move from general disagreement about status to specific actions: chase the report, confirm equipment return, clarify the delay reason or keep the job on the exception list. I would bring the same approach to performance review meetings by explaining trends, anomalies, data-quality risks and required actions clearly.

Communication and influence are among my strongest transferable areas. At South Western, I supported weekly reporting discussions with operations, equipment, QHSE, admin, commercial colleagues and managers. These colleagues were not under my line management, so I had to influence through evidence, clarity and respect. I presented reporting exceptions through written summaries, dashboard views and verbal explanations, adapting detail to the audience. I avoided blaming language and translated data issues into practical questions, helping colleagues correct records, clarify definitions and take action.

I can also evidence training, supervision and workflow support. At South Western, I supported NYSC students and industrial training students by explaining reporting workbooks, setting small checking tasks, reviewing their checks, correcting misunderstandings around definitions and helping them interpret exceptions before outputs were used. In my current Crew Member role, I train new starters on station tasks, order accuracy, handovers and operating procedures. I also built the acute reporting project with junior analyst handover in mind: a query library, runbook, validation checklist and exception-handling notes so another analyst could refresh reports, check failures, update commentary and escalate unresolved issues.

I am comfortable working independently, prioritising competing demands and maintaining accuracy under pressure. At South Western, I worked to recurring reporting deadlines while handling ad-hoc clarifications and unresolved data issues. I prioritised the most important checks first, separated confirmed information from items requiring clarification, and communicated where data quality affected confidence in the output. I am also able to travel to off-site meetings and would take a flexible approach to service needs.

I understand the importance of confidentiality, information governance and responsible data handling in an NHS information role. For information requests, I would check definitions, date ranges, exclusions, validation evidence, small-number disclosure risk and whether figures should be aggregated before treating them as ready to share. I would follow Trust information security and confidentiality policies, and escalate any uncertainty around disclosure, definitions or sensitive data before release.

I would bring to Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust the ambition to improve reporting processes, the respect needed to work constructively with clinicians, directorate managers, service teams and junior analysts, and the compassion to remember that information ultimately affects patients, women, children, families and the wider population. Accurate reporting, clear commentary, data-quality assurance and timely escalation help services understand pressure, plan capacity, meet national requirements and make better decisions for the communities they serve.
