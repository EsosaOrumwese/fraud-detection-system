# Supporting Statement

I am applying for the Senior Information Manager role because my experience sits strongly across operational reporting, data quality, information assurance, stakeholder communication and the development of reliable reporting outputs. I understand this post is not simply about producing tables, but about supporting a robust corporate information management framework where internal and external reporting is accurate, timely, definition-compliant and useful for clinical, managerial and external decision-makers. My background combines Data Analyst experience, postgraduate data science training, advanced Microsoft Office and database work, dashboard development, statistical analysis, and practical experience influencing colleagues to improve the quality and usefulness of information.

I meet the formal education and specialist knowledge requirements through a First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter, completed with Distinction. My postgraduate study covered statistics, data preprocessing, Python, SQL, modelling, machine learning, visualisation, evaluation and research delivery. It also strengthened my ability to work with complex datasets, interpret outputs critically, and explain limitations clearly. In my MSc research project, I processed high-frequency smartphone sensor data, created model-ready windows, trained deep learning models and evaluated outputs using accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score. This trained me to think carefully about data structure, definitions, representativeness, measurement error and whether an analytical output is safe to use for decision-making.

My strongest workplace evidence comes from my Data Analyst role at South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I supported operational reporting across service activity, equipment movement, QHSE, man-hours, close-out documentation and management information. The work involved Oracle data extracts as structured operational sources, SAS programs for repeatable analytical processing, Excel and Access for reporting inputs and checks, and Power BI for presenting trends, status views and exceptions. I produced recurring reporting outputs showing which jobs were active, delayed, field-completed, awaiting documentation, awaiting equipment confirmation or ready for close-out. This is directly relevant to Hywel Dda's requirement for regular and ad-hoc reports that are accurate, timely, and useful to managers, clinicians and external bodies.

In that role, I regularly worked with complex and sometimes conflicting data from several sources. A job could be marked as completed in one source, but still be missing a signed service report, equipment return confirmation, man-hour record or close-out status. I used Oracle outputs, SAS transformation steps, Access tables and Excel checks to separate raw extracts, cleaned datasets, exception views and final reporting outputs. I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, status logic, inconsistent categories, failed joins and unexpected movements between reporting periods. This gave me a practical understanding of reporting assurance: a report is only useful if the definitions, source data, transformations and assumptions are controlled.

I have strong experience with the Microsoft and analytical tools named in the person specification. In Excel, I have built structured reporting workbooks, pivot tables, lookups, formulas, conditional formatting, validation checks, exception lists and management-ready summaries. In Access, I have used tables, queries and controlled local data structures where a spreadsheet alone was not robust enough for recurring reporting inputs. In Power BI, I have produced dashboards showing trends, exceptions, status views and data-quality measures. I have also used SQL, Python, SAS and Oracle across analytical work, including querying, joining, transforming, testing and preparing data. These tools supported actual reporting processes, not isolated technical exercises.

A key strength I would bring is using information to present a clear argument. At South Western, managers did not need a raw extract; they needed to know which jobs were ready, which were blocked, what was causing delay and who needed to act. One improvement I made was separating "field-completed" from "ready for close-out," because using one broad "completed" category hid important follow-up work. That distinction gave operations, equipment, QHSE, admin and commercial colleagues a clearer view of the work. It helped discussions move from general disagreement about status to specific actions: chase the report, confirm equipment return, clarify the delay reason or keep the job on the exception list.

Data quality and data standards are central to how I work. In QHSE reporting, I learned not to treat missing submissions as true zero values. A service line with low recorded incidents might reflect low risk, but it might also reflect incomplete reporting. I used rates such as observations per 1,000 man-hours, trend views, standard deviation, median and mean comparisons, Pareto breakdowns and completeness checks to improve interpretation. In postgraduate and project work, I applied confidence intervals and ANOVA to compare differences between groups, and used statistical process control to judge whether movement over time reflected normal variation or a change needing investigation. This is relevant to corporate reporting because misleading variation, incomplete data or weak definitions can lead to poor decisions.

I can also evidence reporting system development and documentation through my independent AWS fraud and transaction decisioning platform. Although this was not an NHS project, it gave me strong experience in building reliable reporting and monitoring around changing data flows. I created governed data layers, validation rules, dashboard measures, source-to-output lineage and documentation so that changes in input data could be monitored rather than mistaken for real operational change. The platform included event ingestion, validation, feature creation, rule and model decisioning, case creation, monitoring dashboards and offline evaluation. This experience is relevant to information management because it required the same discipline: define the data, validate the flow, document the measure, monitor exceptions and make outputs interpretable for users.

I recognise that this role requires interpretation of nationally published guidance, NHS data flows, targets, data dictionary definitions and reporting standards. I would not claim direct NHS employment experience, but I understand the reporting discipline behind those requirements: guidance must be translated into clear definitions, inclusion rules, exclusions, mappings, validation checks and repeatable reporting logic. In operational reporting, I have already applied that discipline by defining categories, documenting fields, validating source-to-output lineage and checking whether changes reflected real operational movement or a recording issue. I would apply the same method to Hywel Dda's targets, Welsh Government reporting requirements and local corporate information standards.

I understand the importance of information governance, confidentiality, records management and safe release of information in a Health Board role. My working understanding is that data must be used lawfully, fairly, securely and proportionately, with clear purpose, minimum necessary access, accurate definitions, appropriate retention and documented accountability. For information requests, I would check definitions, date ranges, exclusions, validation evidence, small-number disclosure risk and whether figures should be aggregated before treating them as ready to share. In my platform work, I separated raw events from curated analytical outputs, restricted access to sensitive layers, avoided hard-coded credentials, documented lineage and made validation failures visible.

Communication and influence are among my strongest transferable areas. At South Western, I supported weekly discussions with operations, equipment, QHSE, admin, commercial colleagues and managers. These colleagues were not under my line management, so I had to influence through evidence, clarity and respect. I avoided blaming language and translated data issues into practical questions: whether a service report had been received, whether equipment had returned, whether a delay related to client approval, logistics, documentation or internal follow-up. This helped colleagues correct records, clarify definitions and take action because they could see the operational consequence of the data issue.

I can also evidence training, mentoring and people development. At South Western, I supported NYSC students and industrial training students by walking them through reporting workbooks, Excel checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation. In my current Crew Member role, I train new starters on station tasks, order accuracy, handovers and operating procedures, which has strengthened my ability to break tasks down, check understanding and correct errors patiently. As an early committee member of the University of Exeter Swing Dance Society, I helped organise beginner-friendly activity and support inclusive participation. These experiences support the role's requirements around training, mentoring, teamwork and inclusive communication.

I am comfortable working independently within a framework, managing competing demands and maintaining attention to detail under pressure. At South Western, I worked to recurring reporting deadlines while handling ad-hoc clarifications and unresolved data issues. During my MSc, I balanced lectures, technical projects, research deadlines and written outputs. In building the AWS platform, I planned and delivered a long-running technical project with documentation, testing, monitoring and iteration. These experiences have taught me to prioritise the most important checks first, make uncertainty visible, and escalate or clarify when a decision depends on incomplete information.

I would bring to Hywel Dda University Health Board a strong combination of reporting discipline, analytical depth, stakeholder influence and values-led communication. I am used to learning operational systems by tracing how fields, statuses, definitions, source records and validation checks affect final reporting. I would apply that same discipline to Health Board corporate reporting, national guidance, local definitions and performance requirements. I would also work in line with the Health Board's values: dignity, respect and fairness in how I work with colleagues; integrity, openness and honesty in how I handle data; caring, kindness and compassion in remembering that information supports services for patients and communities; and a commitment to working together to develop excellent services.
