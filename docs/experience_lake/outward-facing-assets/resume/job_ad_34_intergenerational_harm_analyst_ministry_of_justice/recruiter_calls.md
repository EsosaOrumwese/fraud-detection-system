# Recruiter Calls - Job Ad 34 - Ministry of Justice

Role:
- `Intergenerational Harm Analyst`
- `Ministry of Justice`
- `Cross-Government and Academia`
- `BOLD Prevention`
- `Families and Intergenerational Harm`

Using the updated job-ad evidence doc, the Civil Service Success Profiles capture, and the candidate evidence base as source material, the recruiter's calls for this role are:

1. **Manipulated large, complex administrative or operational datasets to generate evidence that supported public-sector, policy, or operational decision-making.**

2. **Worked with linked or linkable data across multiple sources, entities, identifiers, events, cases, or partner-style datasets rather than analysing one flat file in isolation.**

3. **Applied data-linking, entity-resolution, matching, or record-relationship thinking to understand hidden populations, connected risks, or people/entity pathways.**

4. **Understood that matching methodology must be monitored for quality, coverage, bias, missingness, false positives, false negatives, and operational usefulness.**

5. **Designed or contributed to monitoring dashboards or reporting outputs that assessed data quality, matching quality, coverage, trends, exceptions, or operational readiness.**

6. **Applied complex analytical techniques in `Python`, `R`, or comparable tooling to produce insight, visualisations, and decision-support evidence.**

7. **Used analytical judgement to move from complex data outputs to sound conclusions, including clear assumptions, limitations, confidence, and implications.**

8. **Worked with sensitive or high-risk data contexts where ethics, data protection, quality assurance, responsible use, and controlled access mattered.**

9. **Showed understanding of data sharing, data-governance, and public-interest safeguards even where direct formal data-sharing agreement ownership is not claimed.**

10. **Treated analysis as part of real-world intervention or service improvement, not as academic modelling detached from policy or operational impact.**

11. **Produced evidence relevant to vulnerable people, social harm, justice, prevention, safeguarding, or complex-needs populations, or positioned adjacent work clearly toward those outcomes.**

12. **Managed analytical work from scoping and planning through delivery, including quality, timeliness, documentation, and clear completion evidence.**

13. **Worked in, or designed outputs for, multidisciplinary and cross-functional settings involving analysts, researchers, policy users, operational users, or technical colleagues.**

14. **Communicated findings clearly to technical audiences, including methods, assumptions, data quality, linkage logic, and analytical limitations.**

15. **Communicated findings clearly to non-technical audiences, including what the evidence means, what decisions it supports, and what practical action or caution follows.**

16. **Used responsible automation, `AI`, or large-language-model awareness to improve analytical productivity while retaining human judgement, testing, and governance.**

17. **Demonstrated `Changing and Improving` by improving methods, simplifying processes, using technology carefully, and making analytical change practical for users.**

18. **Demonstrated `Making Effective Decisions` by using credible evidence, considering uncertainty, presenting options or implications, and making timely, defensible recommendations.**

19. **Demonstrated `Working Together` through joined-up delivery, shared evidence, constructive challenge, and collaboration across different functions or perspectives.**

20. **Presented as an SEO-level analytical professional: technically credible, ethically aware, delivery-focused, and able to turn complex linked data into public-interest insight.**

The spine of the call here is:

- linked administrative data and cross-government analytical evidence
- vulnerable-cohort, hidden-population, prevention, and intergenerational-harm insight
- data matching, entity resolution, linkage quality, coverage, and monitoring
- data sharing, ethics, data protection, and quality assurance
- Python/R-led complex analysis, visualisation, and decision-support outputs
- AI/LLM awareness used responsibly and under governance
- analytical project management from scoping through delivery
- clear communication to technical and non-technical audiences
- SEO-level behaviours: `Changing and Improving`, `Making Effective Decisions`, and `Working Together`

Truth-boundary note:
- The candidate should not claim direct `MoJ`, `HMPPS`, `HMRC`, `DfE`, offending-data, benefits-data, education-data, prison/probation-data, parental-imprisonment, or formal cross-government data-sharing agreement experience unless separately evidenced.
- The strongest evidence should be translated from governed fraud-risk entity/event/case/label data, matching and linkage-style analytical thinking, quality assurance, responsible AI/tooling, MSc network/policy projects, and operational delivery discipline.
- The final application should sound like linked-data public-interest analysis, ethics, and vulnerable-cohort decision support, not generic fraud analytics and not a pure machine-learning portfolio.
