# M&S Commercial Analyst - Platform Positioning

This document captures the role positioning work for the M&S Commercial Analyst role. The purpose is to keep the resume aligned to the employer's actual hiring problem before translating platform and experience evidence into business-facing bullets.

## Level 0 - Umbrella Employer Problem

M&S is hiring a Commercial Analyst to:

> Use commercial insight and customer analysis to shape the Financial Services loyalty agenda across products and services, creating personalised offers and proposition decisions that reward customers, strengthen brand sentiment, and drive incremental sales.

Why this is Level 0:

- It comes directly from the Summary section of the job ad.
- It captures the role's main purpose: support the development of the loyalty agenda.
- It includes the business outcomes: reward customers, strengthen brand sentiment, and drive incremental sales.
- It includes the operating method at a high level: commercial insights and analysis.
- It keeps the Financial Services context: Credit, ATMs, Insurance, Savings, Travel Money, and embedded credit.

## Level 1 - Broad Employer Outcomes

Under the Level 0 problem, the job ad breaks the role into these broad outcomes:

| Level 1 outcome | Job ad source wording | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Shape Financial Services propositions | "shape the propositions through commercial insights and analysis" | Use analysis to influence what FS products and offers should look like. |
| Develop a cohesive loyalty programme | "joining up loyalty activity across the FS product set" and "create a cohesive, compelling and effective loyalty programme" | Connect loyalty activity across Credit, ATMs, Insurance, Savings, Travel Money, and embedded credit. |
| Design personalised customer offers | "bespoke, personalised offers" and "designing compelling, personalised customer offers" | Use insight from historical offers and customer behaviour to make offers more relevant. |
| Improve customer journeys and communications | "enhance... customer journeys and customer communications" | Use analysis to improve how customers experience and understand FS products and offers. |
| Grow spend, revenue, FS products and M&S sales | "drive incremental sales", "grow spend", "increase revenue", and "grow FS products and M&S sales" | Commercial outcome: increase customer value and product/sales performance. |
| Understand customer behaviour by segment, channel and proposition | "develop a deep understanding of customer behaviours by segment, channel and proposition" | Build the customer insight layer that makes offer and proposition decisions better. |
| Use loyalty-offer performance insight to inform decisions | "provide insight into performance of loyalty offers", "understand trends", and "inform changes to propositions" | Measure what is working, diagnose trends, and guide proposition changes. |
| Partner across Commercial, Sparks, Data/Analytics/AI | "partner with a variety of stakeholders... Commercial, Sparks and the Data / Analytics / AI team" | Turn analysis into decisions with business and analytics stakeholders. |

Level 1 should not be Excel, PowerPoint, or stakeholder management on their own. Those are capability signals. The actual Level 1 outcomes are commercial loyalty, proposition, offer, journey, customer behaviour, revenue/spend, and decision-shaping outcomes.

## Language Rule

Use recruiter and job-description language, but avoid internal analytics shorthand that forces the reader to interpret the point.

Keep M&S-facing wording such as:

- customer;
- loyalty;
- commercial;
- Financial Services;
- products and propositions;
- personalised offers;
- customer journeys;
- customer communications;
- performance of loyalty offers;
- deep dive analysis;
- trends;
- segment, channel, and proposition;
- grow spend;
- increase revenue;
- M&S sales;
- stakeholders;
- Commercial, Sparks, and Data / Analytics / AI.

Avoid internal shorthand such as:

- higher-fit;
- low-fit;
- target signal;
- propensity segments;
- priority-customer coverage, unless rewritten in plain business language.
