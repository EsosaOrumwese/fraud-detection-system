# Supporting Statement - Kernow Health CIC Data and Digital Analyst

**Degree in a relevant subject or equivalent experience in a data analysis role**

I meet this criterion through my MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence, which I completed with Distinction, and through my First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree. The MSc gave me direct training in data preparation, statistical learning, machine learning, optimisation, SQL, Python, visualisation and responsible interpretation of analytical results. My engineering degree also gave me a strong numerical and problem-solving foundation.

A clear example of putting this into practice was my MSc research project: Multi-Task Learning for Driver Identification and Transport Mode Classification. The project used high-frequency smartphone sensor data from the SHL dataset, including accelerometer, gyroscope and rotation-vector data. The task was to build a model that could classify both the mode of transport and the driver, which meant the data had to be prepared carefully before modelling.

I cleaned and structured the raw sensor data, selected the relevant sensor channels, segmented the data into model-ready windows and prepared it for deep-learning experiments. I then developed and evaluated a multi-task learning approach using CNN and recurrent-network concepts to support both classification tasks. I assessed performance using model-evaluation measures such as accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score, while also considering class balance, sampling limitations, generalisability and whether the model outputs could be trusted.

This project showed my ability to take a real analytical problem from raw data through to modelling, evaluation and written explanation. It also strengthened the habits I would bring to this role: preparing data properly, choosing suitable analytical methods, validating outputs, explaining limitations clearly and making sure the final analysis is useful for decision-making rather than just technically complete.

**Strong working knowledge of Microsoft Excel**

I have used Excel extensively for reporting, data validation and operational analysis. In a field engineering and operations analytics role, recurring mobilisation issues arose when tools, accessories, certificates or permits were missed, misplaced or not returned, causing delays and additional costs.

To address this, I developed an Excel-based loadout tracker that combined inventory, mobilisation and return data in one workbook. Using IF, COUNTIF, SUMIF, XLOOKUP/VLOOKUP, conditional formatting, filters and pivot tables, I identified missing items, flagged overdue returns and reconciled equipment against job requirements. The tracker became both a planning tool and an auditable record, improving visibility and reducing mobilisation risks.

I also used Excel for operational investigations. During a tubing-running operation, concerns were raised that some connections may have been forced during make-up. I transferred tally data into Excel, cleaned and structured it, calculated running rates and elapsed times, and created charts to identify changes in work pace. This helped narrow the investigation to specific sections of the operation and supported a more evidence-based review.
