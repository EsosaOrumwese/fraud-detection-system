This file captures the recruiter-side demand for the University of Cambridge `Research and Data Analyst` role so resume and cover-letter writing can answer the call directly rather than reconstructing it from memory.

## Detailed Call List

1. Combined quantitative analysis and qualitative research to produce robust, defensible evidence for strategy, policy, and service improvement.
2. Prepared, manipulated, validated, normalised, and combined large, complex, and diverse datasets so they were fit for reporting, analysis, and decision-making.
3. Produced accurate routine and ad hoc reports that were understandable to non-specialists.
4. Built and maintained dashboards for stakeholder self-service and used dashboards strategically to reduce repeated one-off reporting demand.
5. Analysed large datasets and national sector data to support planning, benchmarking, and wider strategic decision-making.
6. Used external benchmark datasets such as UCAS, HESA, HEFCE, UKRI, or similar sector sources to provide context, comparison, and institutional insight.
7. Conducted mixed-method research using suitable methodologies such as surveys, interviews, focus groups, and quantitative analysis rather than relying on spreadsheet-only analysis.
8. Evaluated interventions or programmes to assess impact, support accountability, and inform benefits analysis.
9. Wrote high-quality analysis and clear recommendations for stakeholders with different levels of knowledge and analytical literacy.
10. Presented complex findings clearly to non-specialists, committees, and working groups so the analysis could be used in practice.
11. Worked independently with judgement and initiative while building effective relationships across teams and stakeholder groups.
12. Improved reporting and extraction workflows through tools and process design rather than only producing outputs manually.
13. Delivered training, guidance, and data-literacy support that helped colleagues use data, analysis, and evaluation more effectively.
14. Acted as a source of expertise and supported communities of practice or capability-building activity around analysis and evaluation.
15. Worked in a high-accountability data environment with awareness of UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act, FOI, confidentiality, and careful institutional data handling.

## Source-Weighted Reading Note

The source material supports the role as research, analytics, evaluation, and institutional communication rather than a standard reporting post. The strongest supported lanes are:

- mixed-method-style evaluation using quantitative evidence plus bounded qualitative evidence
- large-dataset preparation, combination, validation, and reporting-ready reuse
- benchmark-aware strategic analysis and planning support from internal plus comparator context
- committee-ready analysis, recommendations, and non-specialist translation
- bounded accountability and benefits-focused interpretation
- careful handling of complex institutional-style data

The source material supports these more carefully:

- live surveys, interviews, and focus groups
- direct ownership of UCAS, HESA, HEFCE, or UKRI pipelines
- full institutional planning authority
- quantified institutional benefits or strong causal proof
- broad GDPR or FOI operational ownership from the execution reports alone

So resume and cover-letter responses should stay strongest on evaluation, large-dataset preparation, benchmarking, committee-ready communication, and careful institutional data handling rather than overstating live fieldwork or sector-dataset ownership.
