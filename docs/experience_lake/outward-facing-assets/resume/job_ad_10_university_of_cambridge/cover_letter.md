I am applying for the Research and Data Analyst post because it brings together the parts of analytical work I care most about. Alongside my MSc in Data Science with Artificial Intelligence, that has given me a solid base in evaluation, careful interpretation, and the kind of judgement this role requires.

A large part of my work has also been getting complex data into a form that people could actually use. That has meant preparing and checking large analytical inputs, shaping them for repeat reporting, and building outputs that could be reused rather than re-explained from scratch each time. Using Excel, SQL, Python, Power BI and Tableau, I have produced routine and ad hoc reporting for non-specialists, supported dashboard-style reuse, and used comparator evidence to place current performance in context. I have then turned that work into clear briefings and recommendation notes for wider stakeholders. That is why I see a strong fit with the reporting, benchmarking, and committee-facing parts of this post.

One part of the role I find especially appealing is the emphasis on helping other people make good use of analysis. In my recent work, that has often meant explaining findings in plain language, writing summaries that non-specialists could work from, and making outputs easier to pick up again without another long technical explanation. My wider experience has reinforced that. In busy service and safety-conscious environments, I learned how much clear guidance, dependable handovers, and calm communication matter when people are working under pressure. I would bring that same approach to supporting colleagues’ use of data and analysis here.

What I can say is that I am used to working with controlled outputs, confidentiality-aware analysis, and clear boundaries around what can and cannot be claimed. What I would offer Cambridge is not only analytical capability, but a way of working that keeps the chain intact from data preparation through evaluation to reporting and explanation.
