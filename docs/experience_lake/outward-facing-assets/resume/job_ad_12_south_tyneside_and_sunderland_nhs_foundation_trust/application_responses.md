> In my recent BI and analytics work, I demonstrated data analysis skills, statistical thinking, security awareness, and good data administration by taking a broad reporting need and turning it into a trusted management-information output rather than simply producing a report and sending it out. I used SQL, Excel, Power BI, SSRS and Python to work with data from multiple sources, but the most important part was the judgement behind the tools. I checked whether the data was consistent, whether the patterns I was seeing were meaningful, and whether any anomalies needed to be investigated before the output could be treated as reliable. I did not assume that the first result was good enough. I built a prepared reporting base, tested the output, investigated issues, and only then treated it as suitable for wider use. I also treated IM&T security, data protection, and good data administration as part of the analytical process by handling information in a controlled way, making sure it was fit for purpose, documenting logic and changes clearly, and keeping the output structured enough to be checked, repeated, and safely reused. That is the standard I bring to analytical work: not just getting an answer, but making sure it is accurate, defensible, and safe to rely on.
>
> The same work also shows how I communicate complex analysis to non-analytical users. One of the main challenges was that the original request was too broad, so if I had answered it exactly as asked, I would have produced something less useful and potentially misleading. Instead, I gathered the requirement properly, clarified what the user really needed, and reshaped it into a clearer report and dashboard output that gave more practical value. I then explained the result in plain language: what the output was showing, why it mattered, what needed closer attention, and what should happen next. That is how I communicate effectively at all levels. I do not rely on jargon or assume that stakeholders will interpret technical output for themselves. I focus on meaning, implications, and action. I am comfortable speaking to technical colleagues about method and logic, but equally comfortable explaining findings to non-technical users in a way that is clear, relevant, and useful.
> 
> I also work in an organised, structured, and proactive way, and that was especially important in the wider platform work supporting this analytics. As the work moved through design, implementation, testing, refinement, and execution, I kept methods, decisions, and changes documented so the work stayed controlled and could be delivered on time. A key part of this was maintaining implementation notes, recording why decisions were made, and using version control properly so that if an approach was not working well, I could step back to a stable point and continue from there rather than forcing through a weak method. This reflects both my standard of work and my approach to problem solving. I focus on solving the immediate issue, but also on making sure the method is clear, repeatable, and strong enough for future use. I am used to working on my own initiative, managing competing pressures, and adapting my approach when necessary without losing accuracy or quality.
> 
> Alongside my analytical work, my role in a busy customer-facing environment gave me strong evidence of teamwork, interpersonal skills, and collaborative working under pressure. In a fast-paced shift, the team had to work together to open on time, keep service moving, respond quickly on tills, maintain preparation standards, and make sure customers still received a friendly experience. That environment taught me how to communicate clearly and positively, support colleagues when pressure increased, and stay focused on shared goals rather than only my own task. It strengthened my ability to build good working relationships, contribute reliably as part of a team, and maintain standards even when the pace is high. I would bring that same combination of teamwork, communication, discipline, and service focus into this Data Analyst role.
