# Recruiter Calls - Data Analyst, South Tyneside and Sunderland NHS Foundation Trust

As of `2026-04-08`

This file captures the recruiter-side demand for the South Tyneside and Sunderland NHS Foundation Trust `Data Analyst` role so resume and cover-letter writing can answer the call directly rather than reconstructing it from memory.

## Detailed Call List

1. Designed, developed, and maintained reports, dashboards, datasets, and information systems that supported operational and strategic decision-making.
2. Delivered an efficient, timely reporting, data-processing, and visualisation service for internal and external service users.
3. Supported the development and adoption of a strategic reporting platform and data warehouse as the trusted main source of management information.
4. Designed, documented, communicated, implemented, processed, and maintained reporting requirements for defined service areas.
5. Handled complex statistical and analytical queries, investigated whether analyses were robust, and took corrective action where needed.
6. Investigated data anomalies, resolved issues, and applied preventative thinking rather than only reporting problems.
7. Manipulated and analysed data using spreadsheets and other analytical tools to turn raw data into valid and purposeful information.
8. Extracted data from multiple sources and judged whether it was accurate, fit for purpose, and suitable for reporting use.
9. Validated data quality, performed cleansing and consistency checks, and maintained governance-aware analytical outputs in a healthcare setting.
10. Reviewed and updated analysis methods and documentation when data, processing logic, or reporting requirements changed.
11. Applied data-protection, anonymisation, IM&T security, and FOI-handling requirements as part of the reporting job.
12. Gathered user information requirements and tailored reports, dashboards, and analytical outputs to service needs.
13. Communicated complex analysis clearly to non-analytical professionals, including directors, managers, clinicians, and administrators.
14. Worked closely with services to understand what would add value and offered alternative reporting or analysis options when more useful.
15. Turned business needs and goals into performance measures and decision-support products rather than only producing standard reports.
16. Worked across reporting tools and platform-style BI environments such as SQL, SSRS, Power BI, spreadsheets, and related information systems.
17. Worked independently, maintained a methodical and structured approach, and managed competing deadlines without losing quality.
18. Supported less experienced colleagues through mentoring, buddying, peer review, technical demonstration, training, or induction support.

## Source-Weighted Reading Note

The source material supports the role as a service-facing BI and reporting-platform post rather than a narrow dashboard-production role. The strongest supported lanes are:

- BI reporting-product delivery and trusted management-information use
- reporting-platform and data-warehouse support below full platform ownership
- anomaly investigation, robustness checking, and corrective action
- multi-source data validation, governance-aware handling, and documentation discipline
- service requirement gathering, BI translation, and clearer reporting-option design
- reporting-method improvement, technology optimisation, and repeatable delivery

The source material supports these more carefully:

- live Trust data-warehouse ownership
- full strategic reporting-platform ownership
- whole-Trust governance-office or enterprise-architecture ownership
- broad line-management authority

So resume and cover-letter responses should stay strongest on reporting products, trusted BI outputs, platform support, anomaly resolution, governance, service-facing communication, and repeatable delivery rather than overstating enterprise platform ownership.

## Use Rule

Use the recruiter call as the default response unit.

- If the recruiter reads it as one responsibility, answer it in one bullet where possible.
- Keep reporting products, platform support, anomaly resolution, governance, requirements, and BI-tool language visible.
- Use tool names plainly when relevant: `Excel`, `SQL`, `SSRS`, `Power BI`, `Tableau`, `Python`, `DAX`.
- Avoid repo-centric or internal wording.
- Keep wording outward-facing, fast to scan, and commercially legible.
