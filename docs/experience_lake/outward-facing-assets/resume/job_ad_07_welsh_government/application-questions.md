Delivering at Pace
Maintain accuracy when working with complex or time-sensitive payroll data. Respond flexibly to changing priorities and keep stakeholders informed of progress or issues. Instance 1*
> During a recurring reporting cycle in my current role, I found a material discrepancy shortly before release while working with a large operational dataset. The validated rate on the authorised view was 9.59%, but an alternative denominator reduced it to 4.80%, so the priority immediately shifted from routine output production to checking accuracy and making sure the cycle could still move forward safely.  
>
> I responded by fixing the validation rule, isolating the discrepancy, and rebuilding the reconciliation output so the authorised view could be checked properly against the trusted reference before anything was released. I also kept the relevant reviewers updated throughout, explaining what had been confirmed, what still needed checking, and which version of the output was safe to use. That helped me adapt quickly to the change in priority without creating confusion.  
>
> As a result, I produced a reconciliation-ready output that kept the authorised view within the trusted range and stopped the discrepant version from being used in the live cycle. The full control pack passed all release checks, and I was able to keep the work accurate and controlled under time pressure.

Communicating and Influencing

Communicating and Influencing
Communicate data, issues and findings clearly and accurately. Adapt communication style for technical and non-technical audiences. Instance 1*
> During a recurring control cycle in my current analytics role, I found a material discrepancy in one reporting path shortly before release. The issue was technical, but the immediate risk was practical: the people checking the controls needed to understand why the discrepancy had happened, while the people relying on the output needed a clear answer on which version was safe to use. My job was to explain the issue accurately without creating confusion or slowing the decision. 
>
> I first set out the validation rule and showed that the numerator had not changed, but the alternative denominator had doubled the reporting base and distorted the rate. For the reviewers, I explained the reconciliation logic and why the authorised version remained within the trusted range. For non-technical users, I kept it simple and focused on the practical point: one version had been checked and could be used, while the other needed further review. I kept the updates consistent so both groups understood the issue clearly and knew what to do next.  
>
> As a result, the validated output was retained for release, the discrepant version was not used, and reviewers had a clear basis for the next action. This reflects how I communicate in high-accuracy settings: I keep the explanation precise, adjust it to the audience, and make sure it helps people act on the evidence.
