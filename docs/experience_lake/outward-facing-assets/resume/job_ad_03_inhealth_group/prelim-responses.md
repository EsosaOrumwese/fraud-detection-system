That gave me a maintained detailed dataset of 7,835,199 rows, with 7,835,199 distinct flow_id and 7,835,199 distinct case_id. In practical terms, maintaining it meant not just rebuilding the table, but preserving the grain, the key fields, the field authority rules, and the conditions under which it was safe to use for reporting.

Validation was the next layer. I ran repeatable checks to confirm the dataset was still fit for reporting use. In that example, the validation pack passed 5 out of 5 checks: monthly flow IDs were unique, the truth surface was unique at flow_id, the maintained dataset stayed unique at flow_id, required fields were complete, and the maintained dataset contained only case-opened rows. That mattered because the reporting was only as trustworthy as the maintained dataset underneath it. If the dataset had duplication or field-completeness issues, the reporting would look precise while actually being wrong.

Reconciliation was where I proved that the maintained dataset was not just internally tidy, but externally accurate against the reporting lane it was supposed to support. I reconciled the protected summary back to the monthly reporting output across all four amount bands: less than 10, 10 to 25, 25 to 50, and 50 plus. That was the strongest proof point in the whole exercise, because it showed that once the grain was controlled properly, the maintained dataset reproduced the downstream reporting exactly rather than approximately.

The reporting this protected was monthly operational reporting. Specifically, it protected the same monthly case-open and truth-quality readings that fed the recurring reporting lane. From the maintained dataset, I was able to preserve an overall case-open rate of 9.63 percent and overall truth quality of 19.86 percent, while also preserving the band-level pattern, including the weaker 50 plus segment. So this was directly relevant to reporting accuracy because the dataset stewardship work prevented a structurally unsafe join from inflating or distorting the very KPIs that the monthly pack depended on.

So if you ask how I quantified reporting accuracy, I would not answer that vaguely. A busy month can be mistaken for a failing month if you do not separate throughput, case pressure, and confirmed output quality properly.

That mattered because it ruled out the wrong management conclusion. That is why the trend, risk, and opportunity were important to programme performance. The programme was not just about moving flow through. It was about converting the right work, at the right rate, into outcomes with acceptable quality. The moment one segment persistently consumes more workload than its yield supports, that affects programme efficiency, analyst burden, queue behaviour, and the usefulness of the whole lane. It also meant the recommendation was proportional to the evidence rather than speculative.
