# Senior Data and Information Analyst - CYPMHS Provider Collaborative - Role Interpretation

This is the job-ad interpretation stage only. It captures the role logic, hard sift areas, risk points, and evidence buckets before any CV, supporting statement, or application answer is written.

I have reviewed the job advert, job description/person specification PDF, and functional requirements. This is a **stronger and stricter Band 6 role** than the Amelia stretch example, because the attached person spec asks for **substantial experience in a similar role / Band 5 or above**, plus project/change work, complex stakeholder reporting, analytical statistical processes and senior-level communication.

## 1. What The Role Is Really Buying

This is not just "data analyst who can build dashboards."

The core role is:

```text
A BI / information analyst who can support the CYPMHS Provider Collaborative's Commissioning Hub by producing accurate, meaningful intelligence on pathway and provider performance, assuring data quality, explaining the story to senior/clinical stakeholders, and improving reporting processes.
```

The job purpose says the role supports the analytical function of the Commissioning Hub by delivering business intelligence that informs decision-making and reflects pathway/provider performance. It combines operational understanding with technical expertise, dashboards, reports, data insights, quality assurance, and routine/ad-hoc information support.

So the statement needs to show **BI + performance + data quality + stakeholder narrative + system improvement**, not just tools.

## 2. Essential Criteria The Panel Will Score Hard

The strongest evidence buckets are:

| Sift area | What they need to see |
| --- | --- |
| **Education / learning** | Degree-level education or equivalent, plus evidence of continuous learning. |
| **Similar analyst experience** | Substantial experience in a similar role, ideally Band 5+ or equivalent responsibility. |
| **BI / dashboards / reporting** | Dashboards, performance reports, routine and ad-hoc analysis, Power BI-style reporting, high-quality outputs. |
| **Data quality assurance** | Validation, cleansing, troubleshooting, provider submissions, anomalies, quality-control before publication. |
| **Complex datasets** | Large/complex datasets, multiple sources, contradictory data, credible interpretation. |

The job advert itself also lists essentials around large/complex datasets, spreadsheets/databases, data quality improvement through cleansing and staff training, communication, concentration, health systems/datasets, PowerPoint/Word outputs, independent workload management, stakeholder communication and NHS data experience.

## 3. Desirables And Tie-Breakers

There are a few slight differences between the advert text and the attached person spec, so we should cover both where possible.

Desirables include:

- postgraduate qualification or training with substantial statistical content;
- project management, such as PRINCE2 or similar;
- NHS or Local Authority experience;
- staff supervision/management;
- Power BI, Python and/or SQL;
- extensive experience distilling and presenting key messages to senior staff.

Important point: even though **Power BI/Python/SQL** appear as desirable in the PDF table, the main job duties strongly centre Power BI, dashboards, BI tools and analytics, so we should still treat those as practically important evidence, not optional decoration.

## 5. What Makes This Role Risky Or Demanding

This role has some hard gates. The biggest ones are:

1. **"Substantial experience in a similar role" and "Band 5 or above"** are essential in the attached person spec. That means the statement must not sound entry-level.

2. **"Experience of managing and analysing a wide range of NHS data"** is essential in the advert. If direct NHS data experience is weak or absent, we would need a careful strategy: regulated data, health/public-sector-adjacent evidence, and possibly a claim-bearing NHS public data slice - but we cannot pretend direct NHS operational data experience if it is not there.

3. This is not only technical. It asks for **commissioning/performance/service improvement judgement**: contract metrics, provider submissions, recovery actions, pathway performance, and business intelligence for senior decision-making.

4. The admin/minute-taking/papers requirement is secondary but should not be ignored. It signals they need someone organised, precise and comfortable supporting senior management meetings.

## 6. The Candidate Profile We Need To Build

Before writing anything, we need the evidence bank under these headings:

1. **BI/reporting evidence** - Power BI, Looker, dashboards, reports, stakeholder outputs.
2. **SQL/data extraction evidence** - BigQuery, SQL joins, transformations, reporting tables, Dataform.
3. **Data quality evidence** - validation, reconciliation, missing/duplicate records, inconsistent fields, QA before publication.
4. **Complex data / statistical evidence** - trends, segmentation, baselines, anomalies, monitoring, confidence/variation if available.
5. **Stakeholder communication** - explaining findings to managers/non-technical users, presentations, written reports/memos.
6. **Service/performance improvement** - how the analysis changed a process, reduced errors, improved decision-making, or exposed risk.
7. **Project/change evidence** - platform build, reporting framework, workflow/process improvement, delivery over time.
8. **Governance/confidentiality evidence** - sensitive data, fraud/payment data, data access, careful handling, auditability.
9. **Pressure/prioritisation evidence** - Five Guys, analyst work, platform build, deadlines, live incidents, competing demands.
10. **Health/NHS gap slices** - if needed, a small NHS public-data dashboard or findings note to honestly support health-sector awareness.

## Bottom Line

This is a **credible target only if we can position the evidence at working/practitioner level**, not beginner level.

The supporting statement will need to argue:

```text
I can produce reliable BI from complex data, validate and explain it clearly, work with stakeholders, improve reporting processes, manage competing requests, and handle sensitive information responsibly - with enough health/NHS awareness to transfer into CYPMHS commissioning and provider performance.
```

That is the selection argument we would need to prove.

So the thesis is a strong **technical/research evidence block**, but it cannot carry the whole application.

## Thesis / Research Project

**Project title:**  
**Multi-Task Learning for Driver Identification and Transport Mode Classification**

## What I Did

For my MSc research project, I developed a multi-task deep learning model to classify both transport mode and driver identity using smartphone sensor data. The project used accelerometer, gyroscope and rotation-vector sensor data from the SHL dataset, with a focus on transforming raw high-frequency sensor readings into model-ready inputs.

A major part of the work involved preprocessing. I downsampled high-frequency 100Hz data to 5Hz, reducing the working dataset from roughly 81.9 million relevant records to around 4.1 million records. I then created journey segments and sub-segments so the models could learn from meaningful time windows rather than treating a full day's movement as one continuous block.

I used Python and PyTorch to build and test deep learning models, drawing on CNN, GRU, biLSTM and multi-task learning approaches. The project required me to make decisions about window length, feature representation, model inputs, class labels, sensor selection and evaluation. I also documented the research aim, dataset, preprocessing stages, model approach and limitations.

## Why This Matters For The CYPMHS Role

This gives evidence for:

- postgraduate-level data science;
- complex dataset handling;
- Python technical ability;
- research methods;
- statistical and modelling judgement;
- working with noisy real-world sensor data;
- independent project delivery;

## 1. Peak-Time Prioritisation Scenario

### Situation

I was working a Friday evening shift at Five Guys during a very busy period. We had customers ordering at the till, delivery drivers arriving for collections, several large orders on the screen, and the team was also trying to keep fries, toppings and drinks stocked. ### Task

My task was to keep my station moving without losing accuracy or slowing down the team. I had to serve customers, help with order flow, respond to delivery collections, and make sure I did not ignore basic standards such as checking order numbers, keeping the area clean and communicating delays properly.

The shift stayed pressured, but my section did not become disorganised. Orders kept moving, customers were given clearer updates, and I avoided handing over orders before they were complete. The main thing I learned was how important it is to stay calm, identify the task that affects service most immediately, and communicate early when a delay is starting to build.
