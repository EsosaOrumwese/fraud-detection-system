# Sample Candidate Profile - North London CYPMHS Senior Data and Information Analyst

This is a fresh Amelia, built specifically for the **North London NHS Foundation Trust Senior Data and Information Analyst - CYPMHS Provider Collaborative** role.

The candidate is kept closer to Esosa's level: recent MSc Data Science graduate, currently working in a frontline food-service role, with some data/project/internship evidence but **not** already an NHS Band 5 analyst.

The job itself is buying someone who can produce meaningful BI for the Commissioning Hub, reflect pathway/provider performance, develop dashboards and reports, assure data quality, support routine/ad-hoc information requests, and explain complex information clearly to stakeholders.

The harder person-spec risk is that it asks for substantial similar experience, Band 5+ equivalent experience, report writing, presentations, change/process improvement, complex information projects, analytical/statistical processes and stakeholder work.

# Fictional Candidate Profile: Amelia Hughes

For this role, Amelia is a **stretch candidate**. Her strongest argument is not "I already do this exact NHS job." Her argument is:

```text
I can combine data science, BI reporting, data quality, statistical analysis, operational understanding and clear communication, and I have enough health/public-service-adjacent evidence to transfer into a CYPMHS commissioning BI environment.
```

That is the story her profile has to support.

# Concrete Project Evidence

## 1. MSc Capstone Project: CYPMHS Provider Performance BI Prototype

**Project title:**  
**"Building a Power BI prototype for children and young people's mental health provider performance monitoring"**

This was a university capstone project. It used public NHS mental-health statistics, open population data and synthetic provider-level extracts. It did **not** use real patient-identifiable data and was not commissioned by the NHS.

## What Amelia Did

Amelia created a small provider-performance dataset with fields for:

- provider;
- month;
- referral source;
- age band;
- referral count;
- accepted referrals;
- first-contact wait;
- open caseload;
- missed appointments / DNA;
- discharge status;
- data-quality flags.

She used **SQL** to join monthly provider extracts, **Python** to clean missing values and generate summary measures, and **Power BI** to build a dashboard showing referral volume, first-contact waiting-time bands, DNA rates, open cases, provider comparisons and data-quality exceptions.

She also built a basic **SPC chart** to show whether waiting-time movement was normal variation or a signal requiring review. One finding was that one provider appeared to have deteriorating first-contact waits, but the issue was partly affected by missing assessment dates in two reporting months. Amelia documented this as a data-quality limitation rather than presenting it as a purely operational failure.

## Why This Matters For The Job

This gives her evidence for:

- CYPMHS-relevant analysis;
- provider performance thinking;
- Power BI dashboarding;
- SQL and Python;
- data quality;
- SPC/statistical thinking;
- clear explanation of assumptions and limitations;
- turning complex data into usable intelligence.

This is her strongest job-aligned evidence.

## 2. Data Quality Slice: Provider Submission Validation Workbook

**Project title:**  
**"Provider submission quality-check workbook"**

This was a self-directed portfolio slice built after Amelia read job adverts asking for data quality, provider submissions and validation.

## What Amelia Did

She created a small Excel/Power Query workbook that checked a synthetic provider submission for:

- duplicate referral IDs;
- missing provider codes;
- invalid dates, such as contact date before referral date;
- missing age bands;
- inconsistent DNA labels;
- blank outcome fields;
- sudden month-on-month activity changes.

The workbook produced a "quality issues" tab and a summary table showing issue counts by provider and field. Amelia also wrote a one-page data-quality note explaining which errors were critical, which were warning-level, and which needed provider clarification.

## Why This Matters For The Job

This gives her evidence for:

- Excel;
- data validation;
- cleansing;
- quality assurance;
- provider-style reporting;
- documentation;
- attention to detail.

It does not prove she has managed real NHS provider submissions, but it gives her a truthful claim-bearing slice around data quality.

## Concrete Situation

She did not just produce a dashboard. She showed why the data could not be trusted until the definitions were aligned.

## 5. Nando's Operational Improvement Evidence

## Concrete Situation

During evening shifts, Amelia noticed that delivery order problems were more common on Fridays and Saturdays, especially missing sauces, cold side items and orders waiting too long before courier collection.

She suggested keeping a simple shift log for three weeks. The log tracked:

- time order was completed;
- courier collection time;
- whether the order was checked before sealing;
- missing-item complaints;
- remake reasons.

The shift lead used the log to change the way delivery orders were staged during peak periods. Staff began checking sauces and sides before sealing bags, and completed orders were moved to a clearer collection area.

## Why This Matters For The Job

This gives her evidence for:

- noticing repeated operational issues;
- collecting simple data;
- improving a process;
- working under pressure;
- communicating with colleagues;
- supporting quality and service standards.

This is not "data analyst" evidence in a formal sense. But it shows Amelia can connect data, process and service delivery in a real environment.

# Realistic Hiring-Panel Position

For this specific **Senior Data and Information Analyst - CYPMHS Provider Collaborative** role, Amelia is a **high-risk stretch candidate**.

A strict panel may reject her because the person specification asks for substantial similar experience and Band 5+ equivalent experience. The advert also asks for experience managing and analysing a wide range of NHS data. Amelia does not honestly have that.

But her application would not be random. She has a coherent argument:

```text
Amelia is an early-career data candidate with MSc-level data science training, Power BI/SQL/Python evidence, data-quality examples, provider-style dashboard work, health/public-service-adjacent project experience, and frontline operational evidence from Nando's. She lacks direct NHS/Band 5 experience, but she can show that she understands the shape of the work: accurate BI, data quality, pathway performance, clear narratives, and service improvement.
```

That is the useful learning point.

This version of Amelia shows the type of candidate profile we want: not a giant questionnaire, not a CV dump, but a **believable evidence story** with strong evidence, partial evidence, and honest gaps.
