## Critical Assessment Of The Job Ad

This is a **Band 6 Data & Insights Analyst** role, but the advert is written in a slightly friendly/open way: “whether you are an experienced analyst or looking to take the next step in your data career.” That wording makes the role sound accessible. But the attached job description is heavier than the advert. It is not just asking for someone who can make dashboards. It is asking for someone who can support **trust-wide decision-making, reporting, transformation, data flows, statistical interpretation, governance and service improvement**.

The role purpose is the clearest signal: they want someone who can **triangulate data from multiple sources** to inform data-driven decisions, and provide analysis for operational management, service improvement, contractual analysis, business planning and transformation. That means this is an insight/decision-support role, not a narrow reporting assistant role.

## What They Are Really Looking For

They are looking for someone who can sit between **data, services, clinicians, managers and operational decisions**.

The advert repeatedly says the analyst will work with clinicians, managers and stakeholders, analyse and present data from different sources, support performance monitoring, service improvement and planning, and translate complex information into meaningful insight for non-technical audiences.

So the core requirement is not simply technical skill. It is:

```text
Can this person turn complex healthcare data into safe, useful, understandable information that helps services make better decisions?
```

## The Main Scoring Themes

### 1. Analysis That Supports Service Decisions

They want analysis that has a purpose: operational management, service improvement, contract reporting, business planning and transformation. The JD specifically asks for findings colleagues can understand and use, awareness of how analysis supports NHS service delivery, awareness of patient impact, and understanding of how results will be perceived in the business context.

That tells us they are not looking for “I like data.” They are looking for judgement: knowing what the analysis is for, who will use it, and what decisions it may influence.

### 2. Communication Between Technical And Non-Technical Audiences

This is a major theme. The JD asks for clear written ideas, unambiguous description of data, checking that others have understood, shaping communication to the audience, awareness of bias/misunderstood terms, translating technical concepts, and presenting recommendations.

So they want someone who can avoid dumping technical detail on service managers. The analyst must interpret, simplify, explain, and influence without distorting the data.

### 3. Reporting, Data Flows And Automation

The main duties include leading implementation of data flows for new reporting requirements, liaising with data preparation colleagues in the digital division, supporting national/local contract reporting, reducing manual processes, and creating automated solutions to information challenges.

This suggests the Trust has recurring reporting burdens and wants better processes, not just one-off analysis. “Automated solutions” and “data flows” are important phrases here.

### 4. Data Quality, Validation And Governance

The JD is heavy on data quality. It asks for extracting data from multiple sources, checking whether data is accurate and fit for purpose, understanding relational structures, identifying data quality issues, cleansing data, consistency checks, anonymisation, documentation, version control, and quality assurance after changes to data or processing.

This is one of the biggest hidden requirements. They do not just want someone to visualise data. They want someone who understands that healthcare data can be messy, sensitive, incomplete, badly coded, or misleading if not checked.

### 5. Statistical Judgement, Not Just Statistics Vocabulary

The person specification names “advanced statistical techniques and modelling” and refers to the NHS Making Data Count Programme. The JD expands this into practical requirements: inferential statistics, basic regression, analysis across three or more groups, normal and special cause variation, SPC charts, XmR charts, variability, time lags in evaluation, prevalence/incidence, predictive analytics, and longitudinal analysis.

The key phrase in the advert is that statistical techniques should ensure “conclusions are appropriate for sample size and variation.”

That means they are concerned about people drawing bad conclusions from noisy data. They want someone who understands variation, sample size, uncertainty and whether a change is meaningful.

### 6. Population Health And Inequalities

The advert says the analyst will identify and develop processes to analyse data sources that help the Trust understand its population, including potential health inequalities, to inform future service provision.

This is not just generic analytics. The Trust serves mental health, physical health, community, inpatient, neurological, rehabilitation, urgent community response, hospital-at-home and specialist services across Hampshire and Isle of Wight. So “population” here means understanding service need across different communities, geographies and patient groups.

### 7. Project / Change / Process Improvement

The person specification asks for flexibility, responding to change, and identifying/developing/improving processes. The JD also includes project management: keeping work in scope, reporting progress, advising on project plans, leading pilot/testing programmes, and supporting implementation/sustainability.

So this role has a change/transformation flavour. They need someone who can work in moving conditions, not just maintain a fixed report.

### 8. Freedom To Act And Band 6 Maturity

The “freedom to act” section matters. The postholder manages their own workload, assesses priorities with the Lead Analyst, manages customer expectations, keeps up with national reporting requirements, ensures compliance with information governance, and may deputise for senior colleagues at Trust and Commissioner-level meetings.

That tells us this is not a passive junior analyst role. Even if they are open to someone taking the next step, they expect Band 6 maturity: judgement, ownership, prioritisation and professional communication.

### 9. Values, Equality, Governance And NHS Constitution

The Trust’s CARE values are explicit: **Compassion, Accountability, Respect and Excellence**. The person specification also expects professional support for frontline clinicians, commitment to equality/diversity/inclusion, and an honest, conscientious, proactive approach.

The later pages add the governance environment: information governance, confidentiality, records management, data protection, information security, and the NHS Constitution duty to be honest and truthful.

So values are not decoration here. They connect directly to safe handling of sensitive health data and responsible reporting.

## The Employer’s Hidden Problem

The hidden problem is not:

```text
We need someone who can use Power BI.
```

It is closer to:

```text
We need someone who can help a large, complex healthcare trust make better operational and strategic decisions by producing reliable, governed, statistically sound, clearly explained insight from multiple data sources, while reducing manual reporting and supporting service improvement.
```

That is the centre of the role.

## The Critical Warning From The Advert

The advert sounds broad and encouraging, but the JD is demanding. The short person specification underplays the actual depth of the role. The attached JD carries the real expectations: data flows, automation, governance, advanced statistics, data visualisation testing, project work, national reporting, FOI, contract reporting, and possibly deputising or professional leadership.

So we should read this as a **Band 6 analytical decision-support role inside a healthcare reporting/transformation environment**, not as a simple BI/dashboard vacancy.
