Using the updated job-ad evidence doc, the ideal candidate profile, and the application-materials operating guide as source material, the recruiter's calls for this role are:

1. **Applied appropriate statistical methods to structured datasets to generate credible evidence for strategic decision-making.**

2. **Worked with research-system, higher-education, grant, bibliometric, survey, policy, or comparable structured datasets rather than only generic business data.**

3. **Produced analysis that helped decision makers understand a wider landscape, system, or ecosystem rather than only reporting local operational performance.**

4. **Identified trends, emerging developments, knowledge gaps, weak signals, and opportunities for deeper exploration.**

5. **Contributed to monitoring, horizon scanning, foresight, or situational-awareness work that helped leaders understand what may matter next.**

6. **Used `Python`, `SQL`, `R`, or comparable analytical tooling to prepare, analyse, visualise, and interpret structured data.**

7. **Showed strong analytical and problem-solving judgement, including method choice, assumptions, limitations, data quality, and cautious interpretation.**

8. **Synthesised complex information clearly and concisely so non-specialist and specialist audiences could understand the key message quickly.**

9. **Produced landscape summaries, dashboards, visualisations, analytical reports, briefings, or presentations tailored to different decision needs.**

10. **Communicated analytical outputs to varied audiences, including internal stakeholders, external stakeholders, or strategic decision makers.**

11. **Worked independently in ambiguous or developing analytical settings, shaping useful analytical activity rather than waiting for fully specified tasks.**

12. **Managed competing priorities while still delivering timely, accurate, and decision-useful outputs.**

13. **Supported organisational capability in intelligence gathering, data-informed decision making, or analytical ways of working.**

14. **Applied survey-design or survey-analysis understanding where relevant, including limitations, bias, question design, or interpretation discipline.**

15. **Connected descriptive analysis to interpretive and strategic insight, explaining not only what changed but why it may matter.**

16. **Presented as a strategic intelligence analyst: rigorous with data, concise in synthesis, comfortable with horizon scanning, and credible in research or public-policy evidence contexts.**

The spine of the call here is:

- concise synthesis for strategic decision makers
- dashboards, reports, briefings, presentations, and visualisations
- independent working in a new analytical function
- competing-priority management and timely delivery
- organisational capability building in data-informed decision making
