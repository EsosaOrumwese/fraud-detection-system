
**1. Why do you feel that this is the right role for you?**

This role feels like a strong fit because it sits in the area where I work best: taking complex operational data and turning it into clear management information, practical recommendations, and reporting that helps teams make better decisions quickly. What appeals to me most is that it is not just a dashboard role. It is about understanding how the operation actually works, spotting inefficiencies or pressure points, and then giving people something useful to act on.

That matches the way I like to work. I am at my best when analysis is closely tied to operational improvement rather than being treated as an output on its own. I also like the fact that the role combines stakeholder-facing MI with practical tool use across Power BI, Tableau, SQL, Python or R, and cloud-based data platforms, because that is the kind of environment I have been working in and want to keep building in.

**2. Please outline the skills and experience you believe you could bring to this role.**

I would bring a mix of operational analytics, stakeholder-focused reporting, and practical delivery in fast-moving environments.

Technically, my strongest tools are Power BI, Tableau, SQL, Python, R, Excel, Power Query, and DAX. I use them to build dashboards, MI, and analytical outputs around business-user needs rather than generic reporting templates. I have also worked across cloud and enterprise data environments including Azure Data Lake, Azure Data Factory, Databricks, SQL Server, and Oracle-style settings, using them to access, link, analyse, and troubleshoot complex data.

I would also bring strong analytical judgement. My recent work has involved interrogating large operational datasets to identify trends, anomalies, inefficiencies, and improvement opportunities, then turning those findings into practical recommendations. One example was operational impact analysis where I translated a strategy change into a smaller, cleaner review population, reducing selected-flow burden by 511,500 and downstream case-handling activity by 1,185,849 while slightly improving confirmed-outcome concentration. That kind of work matters to me because it shows how analysis can improve prioritisation and reduce avoidable effort rather than simply produce a report.

I am also used to managing multiple requests at once, prioritising well, and keeping quality high under pressure. A big part of how I work is improving the way analytics is produced, not just the final output. That includes automation, reusable data assets, and cleaner workflows that make recurring work faster and easier to maintain.

Finally, I work well with stakeholders. I am comfortable partnering with operational teams, understanding what they are really asking for, and translating analysis into something clear enough to act on. I do not see analytics as a separate technical function. I see it as something that should help the business understand what is happening and what to do next.
