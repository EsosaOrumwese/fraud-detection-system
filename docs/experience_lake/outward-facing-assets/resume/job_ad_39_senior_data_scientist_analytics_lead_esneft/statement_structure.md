> Demonstrate that you have read the published person specification and how you meet the essential and, where relevant, desirable criteria for this post. Include reasons for applying and highlight your talents and strengths. Do not duplicate personal details already provided elsewhere.

>

>
> i. The reasons why you are applying for the post.
> ii. Details of any relevant experience gained either in a paid or voluntary capacity.

> iv. Details of Personal Attributes you have to support your application.

## Word Limit and Internal Target

Employer limit:

- Not confirmed from the extracted advert evidence.

Internal target:

- `850-950` words unless the form gives a lower limit.

Reason:

- This is a Band 8a NHS role with many essential criteria across advanced analytics, technical skill, project delivery, quality assurance, communication, and leadership.
- The statement needs enough detail to be markable but should not become a long essay.

## Main Story in One Sentence

I offer strong advanced analytics, machine learning, forecasting, reproducible delivery, and quality-assurance evidence, with enough project ownership and service-improvement mindset to contribute to ESNEFT's Advanced Analytics Team, while being honest that my strongest leadership evidence is project and capability contribution rather than formal NHS line management.

## Role Classification

`Stretch Fit`

Reason:

- Strong match on data science, Python, SQL, forecasting, ML, quality assurance, documentation, and MSc-level analytical capability.
- Material gaps around direct NHS analytics leadership, Band 8a management, Epic EPR, Azure Data Lake, line management of analysts, and direct operational/clinical stakeholder ownership.

How to handle:

- Do not apologise for gaps.
- Do not claim direct NHS leadership.
- Lead with the strongest technical and project evidence.
- Use honest language around readiness to contribute, quality assurance, knowledge sharing, and developing capability.

## Paragraph Plan

### Paragraph 1 - Reasons for Applying and Role Understanding

Purpose:

- Name the role and ESNEFT.
- Show understanding of the Advanced Analytics Team and `Time Matters`.
- State why the role fits: advanced analytics that helps services plan, reduce delay, and make better decisions.

Criteria covered:

- reason for applying
- service improvement motivation
- communication of role understanding
- alignment with ESNEFT's `Time Matters` philosophy
- prompt item i

Evidence source:

- role advert and Trust context
- candidate's preference for applied analytics that supports operational decisions

Truth boundary:

- Do not write as if the candidate has already worked inside ESNEFT or NHS analytics.

### Paragraph 2 - Relevant Experience: Advanced Analytics, Machine Learning, and Forecasting

Purpose:

- Prove the core technical criterion early.
- Show Python/SQL, predictive modelling, statistical modelling, supervised methods, forecasting, model evaluation, and practical outputs.

Criteria covered:

- prompt item ii
- analytical products and insights
- predictive analyses
- visual dashboards
- Python and SQL
- machine-learning algorithm development
- modelling and forecasting

Evidence source:

- open-source fraud risk intelligence platform
- 7.31% test MAPE forecast
- 2.29x baseline yield prioritisation evidence
- dashboard/KPI monitoring outputs

Truth boundary:

- Do not imply NHS waiting-list or clinical model delivery.
- Use operational/demand/planning language without falsely making it healthcare-specific.

### Paragraph 3 - Training, Education, and Advanced Methods

Purpose:

- Prove Master's-level numerical/data science grounding.
- Show deep learning, classification, large dataset preparation, public-health analysis, network analysis, optimisation, and written technical reporting.

Criteria covered:

- prompt item iii
- Master's-level numerical subject
- advanced analytics knowledge
- supervised/unsupervised methods
- classification, regression/decision-tree knowledge if discussed carefully
- NLP/LLM awareness can be mentioned only briefly if defensible through learning and workflow use
- benchmarking/modelling/forecasting foundations

Evidence source:

- MSc thesis: 81,938,560 raw 100Hz records to 4,096,928 validated modelling records
- multi-task learning for driver identification and transport mode classification
- COVID-19 policy/activity analysis
- network analysis and optimisation projects

Truth boundary:

- Do not claim direct R project delivery unless separately evidenced.
- Use `working R fundamentals` or focus on Python/SQL if discussing tools.

### Paragraph 4 - Quality Assurance, GitHub, Documentation, and Governance

Purpose:

- Show the candidate can produce trusted outputs, not just interesting models.
- This is critical because the role has accountability for analytics quality.

Criteria covered:

- GitHub documentation and validation
- quality assurance of analytical products
- accuracy, accessibility, usefulness
- data quality, validation, governance
- risk-aware analytical delivery

Evidence source:

- Git/GitHub working practices
- modular Python/SQL workflows
- train/test separation
- leakage controls
- schema checks
- completeness/reconciliation checks
- documented assumptions and limitations
- handover notes

Truth boundary:

- Do not claim accountability for a team's outputs.
- Frame as personal QA discipline and readiness to apply that discipline in a team setting.

### Paragraph 5 - Relevant Experience and Personal Attributes: Project Ownership, Prioritisation, and Independent Judgement

Purpose:

- Address medium-sized project management, prioritisation, unexpected demands, autonomous decisions, and delivery discipline.

Criteria covered:

- prompt items ii and iv
- managing medium-sized analytical projects
- project scoping
- project timetable/delivery awareness
- prioritising workload
- independent judgement
- problem solving
- responding to unexpected demands

Evidence source:

- platform work from question framing to output
- deciding what data could answer
- selecting checks before outputs could be trusted
- engineering field experience with escalation and controlled handovers
- Five Guys live service pressure if needed briefly

Truth boundary:

- Do not claim portfolio management inside an NHS Advanced Analytics Team.
- Use project ownership and delivery discipline, not formal programme leadership.

### Paragraph 6 - Personal Attributes: Communication, Influence, and Capability Building

Purpose:

- Show ability to explain complex work and support others' use of analytics.
- Handle leadership and team-development criteria carefully.

Criteria covered:

- prompt item iv
- written and verbal communication
- explaining complex/sensitive information
- persuasion, influence, professional challenge
- developing team capability
- knowledge sharing
- inclusive/collaborative approach

Evidence source:

- explaining model reliability, limitations, operational meaning, and justified action
- documentation and handover notes
- reusable workflows
- service work communication under pressure
- engineering handovers

Truth boundary:

- Do not claim line management, performance reviews, formal staff development, or managing two to four analysts.
- Use truthful wording such as `supporting shared understanding`, `documenting work so others can review and reuse it`, and `sharing analytical reasoning clearly`.

### Paragraph 7 - Interests, Modern NHS Data Infrastructure, AI, and Closing Contribution

Purpose:

- Cover EPR, Epic, Azure Data Lake, LLMs/Copilot, and change/innovation without overclaiming.
- Close with what the candidate would bring.

Criteria covered:

- prompt items i, iii and iv
- EPR / Epic / Azure Data Lake awareness
- emerging AI and automation tools
- desire to improve performance and services
- change and innovation
- reason for applying

Evidence source:

- cloud/data-platform awareness
- Git/GitHub reproducibility
- LLM-assisted workflow design awareness
- open-source platform and MSc advanced analytics work

Truth boundary:

- Do not claim direct Epic, EPR, Azure Data Lake, Copilot automation production work, or NHS systems experience.
- Phrase as readiness to apply existing analytics discipline in ESNEFT's environment.

## Criteria Coverage Map

- Analytical products, predictive analysis, dashboards: paragraphs 2 and 3.
- Python and SQL: paragraphs 2, 3, and 4.
- R: paragraph 3 only as working fundamentals unless the user provides stronger evidence.
- Machine learning algorithms: paragraphs 2 and 3.
- Forecasting, modelling, benchmarking: paragraphs 2 and 3.
- GitHub documentation and validation: paragraph 4.
- Medium-sized analytical project management: paragraph 5.
- Team development: paragraph 6, carefully framed as knowledge sharing and capability contribution.
- Quality assurance and accountability: paragraph 4, with personal QA discipline rather than team accountability.
- Communication and influence: paragraph 6.
- EPR/Epic/Azure Data Lake: paragraph 7 as awareness, not direct experience.
- Time Matters / service improvement: paragraphs 1 and 7.

## Must Avoid

- `I do not have direct NHS experience`
- `Although I lack...`
- `NHS-adjacent`
- `public-purpose link`
- `production-shaped`
- `stakeholder-readable`
- `governance-conscious`
- claiming direct Epic, EPR, Azure Data Lake, NHS clinical data, patient data, or waiting-list analytics
- claiming direct line management of analysts or Band 8a-equivalent leadership
- writing a technical essay that does not show service or operational relevance
- listing tools without saying what was built, checked, explained, or improved

## Final Scoring Check

The final statement is not ready unless:

- each essential criterion has visible coverage
- each paragraph has a real evidence source
- tools are attached to actions and outputs
- technical evidence is linked to service, planning, quality, delay, staff time, patient experience, or operational decision-making
- leadership is framed honestly as project ownership, quality discipline, communication, documentation, and capability contribution
- no sentence invents direct NHS leadership or clinical systems experience
- the statement sounds like a person explaining real work, not an agent using internal labels
