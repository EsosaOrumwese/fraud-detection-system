# Supporting Statement - ESNEFT Senior Data Scientist-Analytics Lead

I am applying for the Senior Data Scientist-Analytics Lead role at East Suffolk and North Essex NHS Foundation Trust (ESNEFT) because it matches the part of my work I want to build my career around: using advanced analytics, machine learning, forecasting, and clear reporting to help services make better decisions. I am particularly interested in the Trust's Time Matters approach because it gives data science a practical purpose: helping staff understand pressure, reduce avoidable delay, improve planning, and make better use of the information already available to them.

My most relevant experience comes from building and analysing an open-source fraud risk intelligence platform, where I worked with complex operational data using Python, SQL, statistical modelling, forecasting, validation checks, and outputs that could feed reporting and dashboard use. I developed demand-planning outputs with a 7.31% test MAPE and built supervised modelling and prioritisation outputs that produced a 2.29x baseline yield in the highest-risk band. The important point was not the metric in isolation. I used the analysis to explain what the model showed about workload, demand pressure, exception handling, and operational risk, so that the outputs could support planning and review rather than remain as technical model results.

I also produced KPI and monitoring outputs covering data integrity, model yield, case flow, exception signals, demand pressure, and operational risk. This involved deciding what needed to be measured, building repeatable Python and SQL workflows, checking whether the outputs were complete and consistent, and presenting the results in a way that could be understood without reading the underlying code. That experience is directly relevant to producing predictive analyses, dashboards, forecasting outputs, and analytical products that are useful to operational teams. The working discipline I would bring to ESNEFT is clear: understand the service question, test the data properly, model carefully, explain the limitations, and turn the result into something usable.

My training and education are also relevant to the post. I completed an MSc in Data Science with Artificial Intelligence with Distinction, covering machine learning, optimisation, network analysis, public-health analysis, and technical reporting. In my thesis, I prepared 81,938,560 raw 100Hz sensor records into 4,096,928 validated modelling records for multi-task learning on driver identification and transport mode classification. That work required careful data preparation, feature engineering, classification modelling, evaluation, and explanation of results. I also completed COVID-19 public-health policy and activity analysis, combining 1,004 daily CSV files in Python into 2,396,549 observations across 220 countries and linking activity data with policy context. These examples support my suitability for advanced analytics work involving complex data, public-service context, modelling, and written explanation.

I take quality assurance seriously because analytical outputs only matter if they can be trusted. In my applied analytics work, I used Git and GitHub working practices, modular Python and SQL workflows, train/test separation, leakage controls, schema checks, completeness checks, reconciliation, duplicate review, documented assumptions, validation outputs, run documentation, and handover notes. I used these checks to separate source-data issues from transformation, modelling, linkage, control, and interpretation issues before deciding whether an output was reliable enough to use. This is the same discipline I would bring to a Trust environment where inaccurate or poorly explained analysis can affect planning, confidence, and service decisions.

I have also managed analytical work from question framing through to output. In practice, this meant deciding what the data could answer, which datasets mattered, what validation was needed, what trade-offs had to be explained, and how the result should be communicated. I am comfortable working independently, prioritising the work that most affects the reliability of the final answer, and changing approach when the evidence shows that an assumption is wrong. My previous paid field engineering experience strengthened the same habits: accuracy, calm escalation, disciplined documentation, clear handover, and respect for controlled operational environments. Those habits are useful in analytical work because errors often come from rushed assumptions, unclear definitions, or weak communication rather than from the modelling code alone.

The personal attributes I would bring are careful judgement, curiosity, attention to detail, clear communication, and a practical service-improvement mindset. I have translated complex model results into plain explanations of reliability, limitations, operational meaning, and justified next steps. I do not see documentation as an administrative extra; I use it to make analytical work easier to review, repeat, challenge, and improve. Through documented workflows, validation notes, and handover material, I make the reasoning behind analytical outputs visible rather than leaving people to depend on a black box.

I am also interested in the Trust's direction around Epic, EPR data, Azure Data Lake, Python, R, and emerging AI tools. My strongest delivery tools are Python, SQL, Git/GitHub, Power BI and Tableau, supported by working R fundamentals and a strong ability to learn new analytical environments quickly. I have explored how LLM-assisted workflows can support analysis planning, documentation, and automation when used critically rather than blindly. If appointed, I would bring strong analytical judgement, quality assurance discipline, clear communication, and a commitment to making analytics useful for staff, services, and patients.
