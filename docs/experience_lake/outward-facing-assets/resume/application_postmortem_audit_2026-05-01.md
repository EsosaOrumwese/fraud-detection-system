# Application Postmortem Audit - 2026-05-01

Purpose:
- review submitted or prepared application materials after the Met Office rejection
- identify whether the same failure pattern appears elsewhere
- separate role-fit risk, content risk, truth-boundary risk, and external constraints

## Main Lessons

1. The recruiter calls and ideal candidate profiles usually identified the correct target shape. The bigger failure risk is in the final translation: some applications still sounded like strong transferable analytics rather than role-native evidence.

2. The independent platform is valuable, but it should not be presented as if it were a conventional employer. Safer framing is `Independent Data Scientist & Analytics Engineer - Governed Fraud Decisioning Platform` or `Applied Analytics Portfolio - Fraud Decisioning Platform`.

3. Do not self-sabotage by explicitly foregrounding what is missing. Replace confessional lines with positive transfer framing.

4. Do not invent live stakeholders, teams, NHS systems, patient-safety offices, suppliers, or external submissions. If the experience is platform-derived, write about decision needs, analytical demands, reporting outputs, and review conditions rather than claiming actual organisational ownership.

5. For stretch domains, build a small role-native proof before applying. This matters most for climate/defence, NHS patient safety, acute NHS performance, commercial procurement, and private-sector stack-specific roles.

## Future Rule

Before applying, every role should be classified:

- Direct fit: platform evidence maps naturally; apply aggressively.
- Adjacent fit: platform maps but domain gap exists; apply with clear transfer framing.
- Stretch fit: build a small role-native proof before applying.
- Structural risk: visa/security/sponsorship/seniority/domain barrier may block even strong content.

Every final application should pass four gates:

1. Role-native gate: does it sound like this job, not generic analytics?
2. Evidence gate: are there concrete examples, not just capability claims?
3. Truth-boundary gate: can every sentence be defended in interview?
4. Competition gate: would this beat someone with direct domain experience, or do we need a small role-native proof first?
