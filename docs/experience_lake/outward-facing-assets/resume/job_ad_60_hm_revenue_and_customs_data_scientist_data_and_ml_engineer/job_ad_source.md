## HMRC Context

HMRC describes its data and digital environment as vast, complex and full of opportunity, with 50 million customers, 60,000 colleagues and more than GBP600 billion collected to fund UK public services.

The Data Science team sits within HMRC's Chief Digital and Information Office (CDIO). It delivers analytical and machine learning solutions that address operational challenges and improve HMRC effectiveness and customer outcomes.

## Job Summary

The role focuses on working closely with data science teams to ensure the best use of the data analytics platform and to support development of that platform.

The advert highlights opportunities for personal and professional development, including upskilling on tools, approaches and techniques, cross-team development and mentoring.

The role also offers opportunities to get involved in data science projects, working collaboratively with data scientists and other professions to deliver scaled solutions into the business. Development opportunities may be driven by the candidate's interests and ideas as well as by project skill needs.

## Responsibilities

The role is responsible for a variety of data management activities across the platform:

- Collaborating with Data Scientists and Engineers to help identify, test, deploy and utilise new platform capabilities and data sources.
- Developing, scheduling and monitoring jobs for commonly required data transformations and transfers.
- Identifying and considering opportunities for data re-use.
- Oversight of analysis, R/Python applications and APIs deployed on the platform, including GDPR compliance.
- Controlling, facilitating and monitoring access to datasets.
- Creating and managing data virtualisation layers to support data exploitation and analytics.
- Developing and maintaining documentation on the team's data virtualisation and integration processes.
- Being an active member of the data virtualisation community, monitoring the programme for opportunities and potential issues while ensuring data scientists have immediate and performant access to data.

## Essential Criteria

- Ability to design, write and iterate code in R, Python, SQL or SAS from prototype to production-ready, understanding security, accessibility and version control.
- Ability to communicate effectively with technical and non-technical stakeholders, supporting and hosting discussions within a multidisciplinary team.
- Ability to integrate and separate data feeds to map, produce, transform and test new data products using technologies such as Oracle, SAS and Denodo.
- Ability to plan, design, manage, execute and report tests, using appropriate tools and techniques, working within regulations and ensuring that risks associated with deployment are adequately understood and documented.
- Experience in collaborating across teams with diverse skills and interests, and contributing to successful delivery of data science projects to solve business problems.
- Experience of growing own technical capabilities and developing skills in others.

## Desirable Criteria

- Understanding of fundamentals of data protection regulations.
- Experience with version control tools, proficiency in using the command line, and familiarity with integrated development environments (IDEs).
- Understanding of cloud architecture.

The advert states there will be opportunity to develop these skills within the post.

## Technical Skills Assessed

The selection process will assess the following technical skills:

During interview, candidates will be asked one question for each technical skill. The question will relate to the responsibilities of the role and the essential criteria. Candidates are advised to prepare specific career examples and use the STAR technique.

## Application Process

This vacancy uses Civil Service Success Profiles and will assess Experience and Technical skills.

As part of the application, candidates must provide:

- A name-blind CV including job history, relevant skills and key career achievements reflecting the requirements of the role. The advert asks for specific instances and impact rather than an exhaustive list of technologies.
- A 750-word personal statement describing how skills and career experience reflect the essential criteria. The advert asks candidates to use specific examples from their career to demonstrate how they meet the criteria.
- Desirable criteria can be evidenced where applicable, up to 250 words maximum. This is not essential but may be considered where candidates have the same interview score.

At sift, the personal statement and CV will be assessed. HMRC may raise the required score at any stage if a high number of applications is received.

The interview will last one hour, be held by a three-person panel, and take place virtually by video link.
