# UCLH - Senior Workforce Intelligence Analyst Supporting Statement

## Improved Supporting Statement

I am applying for the Senior Workforce Intelligence Analyst role because my experience sits strongly across operational reporting, data quality, dashboard development, stakeholder communication and the disciplined handling of complex data. I understand this role is specifically about workforce intelligence: producing reliable workforce reporting, improving ESR data quality, supporting establishment control, maintaining accurate position and cost-centre information, and helping managers use workforce metrics to make safe, timely and financially responsible decisions.

My education and continuing professional development give me a strong technical foundation for the role. I have strong Maths and English qualifications, a BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University, and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter. Through postgraduate and applied analytics work, I developed skills in SQL, Power BI, relational data modelling, data preprocessing, Python, dashboard design, documentation, data quality checking and responsible interpretation of complex outputs. I have also continued to develop working knowledge of NHS workforce intelligence concepts, including ESR structures, assignments, positions, staff groups, cost centres, WTE, vacancies, starters, leavers, sickness, turnover, reporting hierarchies, establishment data and workforce data quality risks.

My strongest workplace evidence comes from my Data Analyst role at South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I worked in a high-intensity operational reporting environment. I supported reporting across service activity, equipment movement, QHSE records, man-hours, close-out documentation and management information. The work required me to analyse large and complex datasets from multiple sources, including Oracle extracts, SAS transformation outputs, Excel trackers, Access tables, equipment movement notes, service records and QHSE submissions. These records did not always agree, so my role was to check, reconcile and turn them into reliable management information.

The domain was different from workforce intelligence, but the reporting discipline was highly transferable: governed records, clear definitions, data-quality checks, reconciliation, exception handling and stakeholder follow-up. A typical reporting issue was that a job could be marked as completed in one source while still missing a signed service report, equipment return confirmation, man-hour record or close-out status. If this was reported simply as "completed," it created a false view of readiness. I therefore separated raw extracts, cleaned datasets, exception views and final reporting outputs, creating categories such as active, delayed, field-completed, awaiting documentation, awaiting equipment confirmation and ready for close-out. This helped managers understand what was genuinely complete, what was blocked and what required follow-up.

That experience is directly relevant to workforce intelligence because workforce data also depends on accurate definitions, governed structures and reconciliation across systems. A post, assignment, vacancy, staff group, cost centre or reporting hierarchy is only useful if it is accurate and aligned with the decision being made. I understand establishment control as a governed workforce data structure: funded establishment, occupied posts, vacancies, WTE, cost centres, position hierarchies and finance alignment must reconcile because managers rely on them for workforce planning, safe staffing, budget control and performance monitoring.

My direct employment background has not been inside an NHS ESR team, so I would approach UCLH's workforce systems carefully and systematically. I would first learn local ESR definitions, position hierarchies, cost-centre structures, data flows, workforce metrics and reporting controls before advising others. My experience with structured operational data gives me the discipline to work safely with ESR and establishment reporting: understand the source fields, check definitions, validate movements, document assumptions, compare outputs against expected patterns and escalate discrepancies before figures are used. I would apply this approach to ESR BI, local warehouse reporting, Power BI dashboards, Workforce Validation Engine requirements and ESR data-quality checks.

Data quality and governance are among my strongest areas. At South Western, I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, failed joins, inconsistent statuses, unclear service categories, unexpected movements between reporting periods and differences between source extracts and final outputs. In QHSE reporting, I learned not to treat missing submissions as true zero values. A low incident count could reflect lower risk, but it could also reflect incomplete reporting. I used rates, percentages, variance checks, trend views, standard deviation, Pareto breakdowns and completeness checks to ensure figures were interpreted safely before being shared. For workforce data, I would bring the same habit: checking whether movement reflects a real workforce change, an ESR update, a position error, a hierarchy issue, a cost-centre mismatch or a data-quality problem.

I have strong reporting and dashboard experience. In Excel, I have built structured workbooks using formulas, pivot tables, lookups, conditional formatting, validation checks, charts, variance views and management summaries. I have used Access and relational database concepts to organise structured local datasets where a spreadsheet alone was not robust enough. I have used SQL to query, join, filter, transform and validate data, and Power BI to present trends, exceptions, data-quality indicators and drill-down dashboards. In my independent analytics platform work, I created governed data layers, validation rules, dashboard measures, source-to-report lineage and monitoring outputs so that changes in data flow could be distinguished from real changes in activity.

I would apply that approach to UCLH workforce dashboards. Reports on headcount, WTE, vacancies, turnover, sickness, starters, leavers, cost centres, staff groups and establishment movement should help users understand not only the headline position, but also whether the data is reliable, what has changed, where action is needed and whether the movement reflects a workforce issue or a data-quality issue. I would aim to build outputs that support HR Business Partners, Finance, Recruitment, Medical Workforce, Temporary Staffing, Workforce Systems and clinical teams with clear measures, exception views and documented definitions.

I am comfortable balancing competing priorities and working to targets. In operational reporting, I balanced recurring reports with ad-hoc clarifications, unresolved data-quality issues and deadlines. I prioritised the checks that most affected the reliability of the final output, separated confirmed information from items requiring clarification, and kept unresolved issues visible through exception lists and follow-up notes. I am used to working with defined reporting deadlines, completeness checks and performance indicators, where the value of a report depends on timely, accurate figures and clear follow-up on exceptions. I would bring that same approach to workforce KPIs, organisational targets, data-quality measures and urgent workforce queries.

Communication is one of my strongest transferable areas. At South Western, I worked with operations, equipment, QHSE, admin, commercial colleagues and managers, adapting the level of detail depending on the audience. I used written summaries, exception views, dashboard outputs and verbal explanations to explain what the data showed, what was uncertain and what action was needed. I avoided blaming language and used practical questions to clarify missing records, equipment returns, service reports, delay reasons and incomplete submissions. I would bring the same approach to workforce stakeholders at UCLH: clear, respectful communication that helps people understand the issue, why it matters and what needs to change.

I can also engage and motivate others. At South Western, I supported NYSC students and industrial training students by explaining reporting workbooks, Excel checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation. I set small checking tasks, reviewed their outputs and corrected misunderstandings before figures were used. In my current frontline role at Five Guys, I train new starters by breaking tasks into clear steps, checking understanding and correcting errors patiently under pressure. These experiences have strengthened my ability to support colleagues with different confidence levels, explain processes clearly and help others work to a reliable standard.

I understand the importance of confidentiality, data protection and responsible information handling in a workforce role. Workforce data can include sensitive information about employees, sickness, staffing, appointments, organisational structures and employment records. My working approach is that data should be used for a clear purpose, shared only at the level required, checked for accuracy and handled in line with policy. In my analytics work, I separated raw data from curated outputs, restricted sensitive layers, avoided hard-coded credentials, documented lineage and made validation failures visible. At UCLH, I would follow local policies carefully and treat workforce data with the confidentiality and professionalism required.

I would bring to UCLH a combination of technical analysis, Power BI reporting, data quality discipline, stakeholder communication and a strong improvement mindset. I would work in line with UCLH's values by putting safety and wellbeing first through accurate workforce data; offering kindness through patient, non-blaming support to colleagues; achieving through teamwork with HR, Finance, Recruitment, Medical Workforce, Temporary Staffing and clinical teams; and striving to keep improving through better dashboards, clearer definitions, stronger validation checks and documented processes. My aim would be to help the Workforce Intelligence team provide trusted information that supports safe staffing, workforce planning, financial alignment and better decisions across the Trust.
