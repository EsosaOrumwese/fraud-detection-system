# UCLH - Senior Workforce Intelligence Analyst Supporting Statement

## Supporting Statement

I am applying for the Senior Workforce Intelligence Analyst role because my experience brings together operational reporting, data quality, dashboard development, stakeholder communication and complex data handling. I understand that this role is focused on workforce intelligence: producing reliable workforce reports, improving ESR data quality, supporting establishment control, maintaining accurate position and cost-centre information, and helping managers use workforce metrics to make safe, timely and financially responsible decisions across a complex Trust.

My education and continuing professional development give me a strong technical foundation for the role. I have strong Maths and English qualifications, a First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree, and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence. Through postgraduate and applied analytics work, I developed skills in SQL, Power BI, relational data modelling, Python, dashboard design, documentation, data-quality checking and responsible interpretation of complex outputs. I have also prepared for workforce intelligence work by studying the concepts this role depends on: ESR structures, assignments, positions, staff groups, cost centres, WTE, vacancies, starters, leavers, sickness, turnover, reporting hierarchies, establishment data and workforce data-quality risks.

My strongest workplace evidence comes from South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where my role combined field engineering and operations analytics support in a high-intensity operational environment. I worked with large and complex records from multiple sources, including Oracle or ERP extracts, SAS outputs, Excel trackers, Access tables, equipment movement notes, service records, QHSE submissions, man-hour records and operational exceptions. These records did not always agree with each other, so my role was to check, reconcile and turn them into reliable management information.

That experience is relevant to workforce intelligence because both environments depend on governed records, clear definitions, reconciliation, exception handling and stakeholder follow-up. At South Western, a job could appear complete in one record while still having missing equipment confirmation, incomplete close-out details, unclear delay descriptions or unresolved maintenance issues. If those issues were not identified, the report could give managers a false view of readiness. I helped separate raw records, cleaned datasets, exception views and final reporting outputs so managers could see what was genuinely complete, what was blocked and what still required action.

Data quality and governance are among my strongest areas. I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, failed joins, inconsistent statuses, unclear service categories, unexpected movements between reporting periods and differences between source extracts and final outputs. In QHSE reporting, I learned not to treat missing submissions as true zero values. A low incident count could reflect lower risk, but it could also reflect incomplete reporting. I used rates, percentages, variance checks, trend views, standard deviation, Pareto breakdowns and completeness checks to make sure figures were interpreted safely before they were shared. The same discipline is relevant to ESR data-quality checks, Workforce Validation Engine requirements and workforce reporting outputs: data movements should be investigated before they are treated as genuine workforce change.

I understand establishment control as a governed workforce data structure. Funded establishment, occupied posts, vacancies, WTE, cost centres, position hierarchies and finance alignment must reconcile because managers rely on them for workforce planning, safe staffing, budget control and performance monitoring. I do not claim employment experience inside an NHS ESR team, so I would learn UCLH's ESR and establishment controls carefully. However, I understand that these are not just labels in a database: they affect vacancy reporting, safe-staffing decisions, cost-centre accuracy, finance alignment and management confidence.

Although my database work has been outside HR systems, I have manipulated structured operational, inventory and reporting datasets where records, statuses, ownership fields, hierarchies and availability indicators had to be accurate before reporting. The same controlled approach is relevant to ESR BI, local warehouse reporting, HR database outputs and establishment-control exceptions: source fields, definitions, movements, assumptions and discrepancies must be visible before figures are used.

I have strong reporting and dashboard experience. In Excel, I have built structured workbooks using formulas, pivot tables, lookups, conditional formatting, validation checks, charts, variance views and management summaries. I have used Access and relational database concepts to organise structured local datasets where a spreadsheet alone was not robust enough. I have used SQL to query, join, filter, transform and validate data, and Power BI to present trends, exceptions, data-quality indicators and drill-down dashboards. In independent analytics work, I also created governed data layers, validation rules, dashboard measures, source-to-report lineage and monitoring outputs so changes in data flow could be distinguished from real changes in activity.

I would apply that approach to workforce dashboards showing headcount, WTE, vacancies, turnover, sickness, starters, leavers, cost centres, staff groups and establishment movement. My aim would be to build outputs that help HR Business Partners, Finance, Recruitment, Medical Workforce, Temporary Staffing, Workforce Systems and clinical teams understand not only the headline position, but also whether the data is reliable, what has changed, where action is needed and whether the movement reflects a workforce issue or a data-quality issue.

I am comfortable balancing competing priorities and working to targets. At South Western, I worked to recurring reporting deadlines and operational indicators such as job readiness, delay trends, QHSE completeness, equipment availability, inventory exceptions and maintenance follow-up. I balanced scheduled reports with ad-hoc clarifications, unresolved data-quality issues and urgent operational questions. I prioritised the checks that most affected the reliability of the final output, separated confirmed information from items requiring clarification, and kept unresolved issues visible through exception lists and follow-up notes.

Communication is one of my strongest transferable areas. At South Western, I worked with field crews, supervisors, HSE, logistics, maintenance, inventory and operations management, adapting the level of detail depending on the audience. I used written summaries, exception views, dashboard outputs and verbal explanations to explain what the data showed, what was uncertain and what action was needed. I avoided blaming language and used practical questions to clarify missing records, equipment returns, delay causes, stock mismatches and incomplete submissions.

I can also engage and motivate others. At South Western, I supported NYSC students and industrial training students by explaining reporting workbooks, Excel checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation. I set small checking tasks, reviewed their outputs and corrected misunderstandings before figures were used. In my current frontline role, I train new starters by breaking tasks into clear steps, checking understanding and correcting errors patiently under pressure. These experiences strengthened my ability to support colleagues with different confidence levels and help others work to a reliable standard.

I understand the importance of confidentiality, data protection and responsible information handling in a workforce role. Workforce data can include sensitive information about employees, sickness, staffing, appointments, organisational structures and employment records. My working approach is that data should be used for a clear purpose, shared only at the level required, checked for accuracy and handled in line with policy.

I would bring to UCLH technical analysis, Power BI reporting, data-quality discipline, stakeholder communication and a strong improvement mindset. I would work in line with UCLH's values by putting safety and wellbeing first through accurate workforce data; offering kindness through patient, non-blaming support; achieving through teamwork with HR, Finance, Recruitment, Medical Workforce, Temporary Staffing and clinical teams; and striving to improve through better dashboards, clearer definitions, stronger validation checks and documented processes.
