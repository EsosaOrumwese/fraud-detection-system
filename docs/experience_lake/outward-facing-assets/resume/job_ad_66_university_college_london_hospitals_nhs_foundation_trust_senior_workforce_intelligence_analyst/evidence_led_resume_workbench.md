# UCLH - Senior Workforce Intelligence Analyst Workbench

## Entry 1 - Role Surface, Scoring Priorities and Fit Warning

This is a Band 6 Senior Workforce Intelligence Analyst role in UCLH's Workforce Intelligence team, sitting inside the Workforce Directorate. It is not a general data analyst role; it is a workforce analytics, ESR, establishment control, position management and workforce reporting governance role.

### Employer Problem

UCLH needs someone who can help protect and improve the quality of its workforce intelligence. The core problem is:

> Can this person produce reliable workforce analytics from ESR, Power BI and the local data warehouse, improve workforce data quality, support establishment control, and give managers, HR, Finance and clinical teams accurate workforce information for decisions?

This is especially important because the Workforce Intelligence team is described as the Trust's central hub for ESR data integrity, position management, establishment control, cost-centre alignment, General Ledger reconciliation, statutory/national reporting and workforce planning.

### Role Identity

The role is best understood as:

> Senior workforce analytics and governance analyst, focused on workforce reports, ESR data, data quality, establishment structures, Power BI dashboards, cross-Trust reporting, and workforce performance targets.

The centre of gravity is not SQL alone, nor general NHS reporting. It is workforce intelligence.

5. Can they produce Power BI dashboards that managers actually use?
6. Can they handle urgent workforce queries calmly?
7. Can they work across HR, Finance, Recruitment, Medical Workforce and clinical teams?
8. Can they deliver against KPIs and organisational targets?
9. Can they explain workforce data clearly to non-analysts?

### Immediate Fit Warning

This is more difficult than the previous East Sussex Band 5 role because several core requirements are specific to NHS workforce systems, especially ESR in an acute NHS setting, NHS/public sector experience, and establishment control. Those are listed as essential or strongly role-central.

So the eventual strategy cannot be "Eric is generally good at data." It has to be:

> Eric is a strong data analyst who can credibly translate his operational reporting, HR/workforce-style database thinking, Power BI/dashboarding, data quality, prioritisation and stakeholder communication into workforce intelligence, while directly addressing ESR, establishment control and NHS/public sector knowledge as the main risk areas.

Before writing anything, decide how much ESR/workforce/public-sector evidence Eric can honestly claim or develop without making the profile sound fake.

It needs to be a workforce intelligence statement: workforce reporting, ESR awareness, Power BI, data quality, establishment control, stakeholder support, competing priorities, KPIs and UCLH values.

The role's centre is clear: UCLH wants workforce intelligence that supports strategic and operational decisions, protects ESR data integrity, improves workforce data quality, supports establishment control, and provides reports/dashboards to managers, HR, Finance, Recruitment, Medical Workforce and clinical/corporate teams.

### Core Argument

> I am a strong fit because I bring the technical and behavioural discipline behind workforce intelligence: analysing large multi-source datasets, building Power BI/reporting outputs, improving data quality, explaining figures to non-technical stakeholders, managing competing priorities, and supporting others to use data accurately. I have developed working knowledge of ESR, workforce metrics and establishment control concepts, and would apply my data-quality/reporting discipline to UCLH's workforce systems safely and systematically.

The wording needs to be honest: Eric should not pretend direct ESR or NHS workforce employment. But the statement should not lead with weakness. Lead with strong transferable evidence, then address ESR/workforce readiness in a controlled way.
