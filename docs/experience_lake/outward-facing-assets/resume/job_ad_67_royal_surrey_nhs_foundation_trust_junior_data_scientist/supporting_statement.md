# Royal Surrey NHS Foundation Trust - Junior Data Scientist Supporting Statement

## Supporting Statement Draft

I am applying for the Junior Data Scientist role because my background is strongly aligned with the Trust's need for accurate, reproducible and meaningful data-science insight to support operational, clinical and strategic decisions. I understand that this role involves working with senior analysts and data scientists to extract, prepare, validate, analyse and model complex clinical and corporate datasets, while producing clear visualisations, dashboards and reports for clinical and corporate teams. My experience combines postgraduate data science training, Python, SQL, statistical modelling, machine learning, data-quality work, dashboard development and hands-on operational analytics.

I meet the degree-level requirement through a First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter, which I completed with Distinction. My MSc gave me a strong technical foundation in Python, SQL, data preprocessing, statistics, machine learning, visualisation, model evaluation and responsible interpretation of analytical outputs. My engineering background also strengthened my numerical reasoning, structured problem-solving and ability to work carefully with complex technical information.

The strongest direct evidence of my data-science experience is my MSc research project on multi-task learning for driver identification and transport mode classification. I worked with high-frequency smartphone sensor data, which required careful preparation before modelling. I cleaned and structured the data, downsampled sensor readings, segmented journeys, created model-ready windows and trained machine-learning models to support two classification tasks. I evaluated outputs using accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score, and documented limitations around sampling, class balance, reproducibility and generalisability. This developed the same habits needed in this role: preparing high-quality datasets, applying appropriate statistical and machine-learning methods, evaluating results properly, and explaining what the data can and cannot support.

I also built an independent fraud and transaction decisioning platform, which gave me practical experience beyond notebooks. The platform was hosted on AWS and included ingestion, validation, transformation, feature creation, rule and model decisioning, case creation, monitoring dashboards and offline evaluation. I used Python and SQL to process data, create features, validate records, score events and evaluate outputs, and used AWS to run the deployed services, store input and output datasets, capture logs, and support scheduled or repeatable processing. I built validation checks for missing fields, duplicates, unexpected categories, failed joins, row-count movements and data-quality exceptions. I also designed dashboards showing event volumes, flagged cases, reason codes, validation failures, score distributions and threshold effects. This strengthened my ability to build repeatable analytical workflows and connect models to outputs that users can interpret.

My workplace data-quality experience comes from South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I supported field engineering and operations reporting. I worked with operational records created close to live service activity, including job start and stop times, equipment status, delays, safety pauses, service records, QHSE submissions, man-hour records and operational exceptions. This gave me experience of data at the point where it is created, before it becomes a clean report or dashboard.

That setting taught me that analysis is only useful when the underlying records reflect what actually happened. I helped turn operational activity into structured records for post-job analysis, reporting and planning, checking whether equipment records matched job requirements, whether maintenance or inventory records agreed with operational reality, whether job notes explained delays clearly, and whether post-job reports showed the difference between planned activity and actual execution. At South Western, I used Excel, SQL logic, SAS and reporting tools to clean, reconcile, analyse and summarise field and operational data; across my MSc and project work, I also used Python for preprocessing, modelling, validation and evaluation. I also supported checks on missing job fields, inconsistent equipment records, incomplete close-outs, duplicate records, incorrect service-line coding and unclear delay descriptions. A missing accessory, incorrect equipment status or vague delay reason could make a report look complete while hiding the actual operational issue, which is directly relevant to healthcare data science where missing or poorly understood data can affect analysis, dashboards and decisions.

I have practical experience using analytical and data-science tools to manipulate and analyse data. I have used Python libraries including pandas for data preparation, and machine-learning libraries such as scikit-learn for modelling and evaluation. I have used SQL for querying, joins, filtering, transformation, validation checks and report-ready outputs. I have also used Excel for formulas, pivot tables, lookups, validation checks, charts, trend views and summaries. Across my MSc, platform and workplace analysis, I have worked with structured datasets, large datasets and data from multiple sources, always checking whether the data is complete, valid and suitable for the question being asked.

I can produce clear visualisations, dashboards and reports. In my platform work, I designed dashboard outputs around user questions rather than chart volume. Headline views showed the main position quickly, while diagnostic views allowed users to explore exceptions, trends, data-quality issues and underlying reasons. In my operational reporting work, I converted field and post-job records into views showing job progress, equipment availability, delay causes, service-line activity, maintenance exceptions and follow-up actions. I would bring the same approach to Royal Surrey: visualisations should help users understand what is happening, its limitations and what action may be needed.

A large-scale data integration and time-series analysis project further strengthened my ability to work with large and complex datasets. I combined over one thousand daily business activity files into a multi-million-row dataset, using Python and pandas to ingest, clean, combine, analyse and visualise time-series patterns. I compared alternative metrics, selected the more appropriate measure for stable interpretation, and presented findings alongside policy context without overstating causation. This developed my ability to perform exploratory analysis, choose suitable metrics and communicate uncertainty clearly.

I am used to making analysis repeatable by documenting data definitions, validation checks, modelling steps and dashboard measures, and by contributing to automated data-processing pipelines so outputs can be rerun, reviewed and improved rather than recreated manually. I understand that NHS data science must be carried out carefully because analysis can affect clinical, operational and patient-facing decisions. Although I have not worked directly with secondary-care datasets in employment, I understand the importance of clinical data quality, information governance, confidentiality, Data Protection requirements and safe interpretation. I would learn local systems, clinical datasets and governance processes from senior colleagues while applying the same discipline I already use around validation, documentation and responsible interpretation.

I work well with others and can explain technical information clearly. At South Western, I worked as part of a diverse multidisciplinary environment, collaborating with field crews, supervisors, rig teams, HSE, logistics, maintenance, inventory and operations management. This experience taught me how to work effectively with people who had different responsibilities, priorities and levels of technical knowledge, and I had to translate operational events into records that office teams could analyse and managers could use for planning, maintenance follow-up and performance improvement. I also supported trainees by explaining reporting workbooks, data checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation. This helped me learn how to explain technical detail without losing the practical meaning of the work.

I would bring to Royal Surrey curiosity, technical capability, care with data quality and willingness to learn from experienced analysts and data scientists. I am able to travel to meetings within the local area when required. I would work in line with the Trust's values by continuously improving analytical methods, excelling with multidisciplinary colleagues, caring about the impact of data on patients and staff, and learning continuously so I can contribute to safe, evidence-based improvement across the Trust.
