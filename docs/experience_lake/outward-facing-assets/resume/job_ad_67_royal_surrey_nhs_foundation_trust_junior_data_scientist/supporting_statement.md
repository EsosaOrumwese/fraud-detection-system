# Royal Surrey NHS Foundation Trust - Junior Data Scientist Supporting Statement

## Qualifications — Essential criteria

I meet the degree-level requirement through a First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter, completed with Distinction. My engineering degree gave me a strong foundation in mathematics, numerical reasoning, structured problem-solving and technical analysis. My MSc developed my practical data-science capability across Python, SQL, statistics, machine learning, data preprocessing, visualisation, model evaluation and responsible interpretation of analytical outputs. Across both qualifications, I produced structured technical reports, explained methodology and limitations, and applied analytical judgement to real datasets and modelling problems.

## Knowledge — Essential criteria

I have strong experience working with structured datasets, including cleaning, transforming and validating data before analysis. My strongest direct data-science evidence is my MSc research project on multi-task learning for driver identification and transport mode classification. I worked with high-frequency smartphone sensor data, cleaned and structured the data, downsampled readings, segmented journeys, created model-ready windows and trained machine-learning models for two classification tasks. I evaluated outputs using accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score, and documented limitations around sampling, class balance, reproducibility and generalisability.

That project gave me practical experience applying statistical and machine-learning methods to a real analytical problem. I considered distributions, sampling, class balance, false positives, false negatives, uncertainty and model performance, rather than relying only on headline accuracy. This helped me develop the habit of explaining what the data and model outputs could support, and where the limitations were.

I have advanced experience using analytical and data-science tools. I have used Python libraries including pandas for data preparation, and machine-learning libraries such as scikit-learn for modelling and evaluation. I have used SQL for querying, joins, filtering, transformation, validation checks and report-ready outputs. I have also used Excel for formulas, pivot tables, lookups, validation checks, charts, trend views and summaries.

I have worked with large and complex datasets from multiple sources. This includes high-frequency sensor data, platform event and decision data, operational records from South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, and a public-data project where I combined over one thousand daily business activity files into a multi-million-row dataset. In that project, I used Python and pandas to ingest, clean, combine, analyse and visualise time-series data, compared alternative metrics, selected a stable measure for interpretation, and presented findings alongside policy context without overstating causation.

I also built an independent fraud and transaction decisioning platform, which gave me applied experience in reproducible analytical workflows. The platform was hosted on AWS and included ingestion, validation, transformation, feature creation, rule and model decisioning, case creation, monitoring dashboards and offline evaluation. I used Python and SQL to process data, create features, validate records, score events and evaluate outputs. I built validation checks for missing fields, duplicates, unexpected categories, failed joins, row-count movements and data-quality exceptions. I also designed dashboards showing event volumes, flagged cases, reason codes, validation failures, score distributions and threshold effects.

My workplace data-quality experience comes from South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I supported field engineering and operations reporting. I worked with operational records created close to live service activity, including job start and stop times, equipment status, delays, safety pauses, service records, QHSE submissions, man-hour records and operational exceptions. I helped turn operational activity into structured records for post-job analysis, reporting and planning, checking whether equipment records matched job requirements, whether maintenance or inventory records agreed with operational reality, whether job notes explained delays clearly, and whether post-job reports showed the difference between planned activity and actual execution.

That experience strengthened my understanding of data-quality issues and inconsistency resolution. I supported checks on missing job fields, inconsistent equipment records, incomplete close-outs, duplicate records, incorrect service-line coding and unclear delay descriptions. A missing accessory, incorrect equipment status or vague delay reason could make a report look complete while hiding the actual operational issue. This is directly relevant to data science because missing, inconsistent or poorly understood data can affect analysis, dashboards, models and decisions.

I understand data visualisation principles and have experience producing clear dashboards and reports. In my platform work, I designed dashboards around user questions, with headline views for the main position and diagnostic views for exceptions, trends, data-quality issues and underlying reasons. In operational reporting, I converted field and post-job records into views showing job progress, equipment availability, delay causes, service-line activity, maintenance exceptions and follow-up actions.

I am used to making analysis reproducible by documenting data definitions, validation checks, modelling steps and dashboard measures, and by contributing to automated data-processing pipelines so outputs can be rerun, reviewed and improved rather than recreated manually. I work well with others and can explain technical information clearly, having worked with field crews, supervisors, HSE, logistics, maintenance, inventory and operations management. I would bring to Royal Surrey strong Python, SQL, machine-learning, data-quality, dashboarding and reproducible-workflow experience, together with curiosity, attention to detail and willingness to learn from senior analysts and data scientists.

## Desirable criteria — Experience of analysing data in a Secondary Care context

My analytical experience has given me a strong foundation that is highly transferable to a secondary-care environment. I have not worked directly with secondary-care datasets in employment, so I would not overclaim this experience. However, I understand that NHS data science supports clinical, operational and patient-facing decisions, making clinical data quality, information governance, confidentiality, Data Protection and safe interpretation essential.

I would approach secondary-care datasets by first understanding the source systems, local definitions, clinical context, data-quality rules and governance requirements. I would then apply the same discipline I use in other analytical work: check completeness and consistency, document assumptions, validate outputs, explain limitations clearly and ensure that any analysis is suitable for the decision it supports.
