# Statement of Suitability

My strongest evidence for this role comes from applying data analysis, issue tracking, control validation, and service-risk thinking to a fraud-risk analytics platform where decisions depended on accurate evidence and disciplined follow-up. The challenge was that risk could sit in several places at once: source data, transformations, model outputs, case movement, reporting tables, documentation, or control logic. My task was to make those risks visible, structured, and actionable rather than leaving them as scattered technical findings.

I used a RAID-style approach to organise blockers, control gaps, validation failures, decision points, action status, follow-up activity, and the evidence needed before work could be treated as resolved. I categorised issues by affected component, likely impact, dependency, data source, control status, and reporting consequence. This helped distinguish routine task tracking from risks that could affect service reliability, assurance, or decision quality.

I then built data-led indicators using Python, SQL, Excel, and dashboard-ready outputs. These monitored decision quality, workload exposure, case flow, model yield, data integrity, and short-range demand pressure. For example, ranked-risk analysis showed that the highest-risk band delivered 2.29x baseline yield, while the top 20.1% of scored flows captured 41.3% of confirmed positives. I also used bounded demand forecasting with 7.31% test MAPE to make expected daily pressure clearer. The result was not just a better analytical view; it gave a practical basis for prioritising attention, planning workload, and explaining trade-offs.

When outputs did not reconcile, I investigated cause before proposing fixes. I checked upstream event records, downstream case movement, labels, reporting tables, schema consistency, source traceability, leakage risk, train/test separation, and repeatable output generation. This allowed me to separate source-data issues from transformation, modelling, control, and interpretation issues. I documented assumptions, validation outcomes, known limitations, and evidence trails so that fixes were reviewable and assurance-ready rather than simply marked complete.

This experience also strengthened my understanding of technical governance and emerging technology risk. I considered data-quality safeguards, access and security implications, explainability, monitoring needs, fairness-aware trade-offs, model-governance controls, and responsible use of AI-assisted coding. That fits a role where technical risk needs to be understood in terms of impact, controls, mitigation, and evidence.

I communicate risk in practical terms. My written outputs focused on what risk exists, why it matters, what control is in place, what action remains open, and what evidence shows progress. In technical field and live service environments, I also developed disciplined habits around accurate readings, escalation, handovers, teamwork, and maintaining quality under pressure. This gives me a strong basis for managing a quality service, working constructively with others, and communicating risk and issue information clearly.
