# Statement of Suitability

My strongest evidence for this role comes from applying data analysis, issue tracking, control validation, and service-risk thinking to a fraud-risk analytics platform where decisions depended on accurate evidence and disciplined follow-up. The challenge was that risk could sit in several places at once: source data, transformations, model outputs, case movement, reporting tables, documentation, or control logic. My task was to make those risks visible, structured, and actionable rather than leaving them as scattered technical findings.

I used a RAID-style approach to organise blockers, control gaps, validation failures, decision points, action status, follow-up activity, and the evidence needed before work could be treated as resolved. I categorised issues by affected component, likely impact, dependency, data source, control status, and reporting consequence. This helped distinguish routine task tracking from risks that could affect service reliability, assurance, or decision quality.

I then built data-led indicators using Python, SQL, Excel, and dashboard-ready outputs. These monitored decision quality, workload exposure, case flow, model yield, data integrity, and short-range demand pressure. Ranked-risk analysis showed that the highest-risk band delivered 2.29x baseline yield, while the top 20.1% of scored flows captured 41.3% of confirmed positives. Bounded demand forecasting with 7.31% test MAPE made expected daily pressure clearer. These outputs turned technical signals into practical risk and service information: where attention should go first, what workload pressure to expect, and what trade-offs needed explaining.

When outputs did not reconcile, I investigated cause before proposing fixes. I checked upstream event records, downstream case movement, labels, reporting tables, schema consistency, source traceability, leakage risk, train/test separation, and repeatable output generation. This allowed me to separate source-data issues from transformation, modelling, control, and interpretation issues. I documented assumptions, validation outcomes, known limitations, and evidence trails so that fixes were reviewable and assurance-ready rather than simply marked complete.

This experience also strengthened my understanding of technical governance and emerging technology risk. I used data-quality safeguards, access and security checks, explainability notes, monitoring assumptions, fairness-aware trade-off review, model-governance controls, and controlled AI-assisted coding so that technical outputs could be challenged, repeated, and explained. This made the evidence more reliable and reduced the risk of treating a model or dashboard output as trustworthy without checking the controls behind it.

I communicate risk in practical terms. My written outputs focused on what risk exists, why it matters, what control is in place, what action remains open, and what evidence shows progress. I apply the same discipline in wider operational settings: checking readings carefully, escalating issues early, giving clear handovers, supporting colleagues, and maintaining quality under pressure. This gives me a strong basis for managing a quality service, working constructively with others, and communicating risk and issue information clearly.
