# Department for Education - Senior Data Analyst (G7) Workbench

## Entry 1 - Role Surface and Scoring Priorities

I have read it. This is a **Department for Education Grade 7 Senior Data Analyst** role in **Schools Digital**, not a standard reporting analyst role. It is a senior analytical leadership role focused on using data across digital services and policy areas to shape decisions, improve services, and support education reform.

## Employer Problem

DfE is trying to use digital services, policy and data together to improve outcomes across the school system: teacher recruitment and retention, SEND reform, school improvement, barriers to opportunity, and better services for children, young people and teachers. The problem is:

> Can this person lead analysis across multiple services or policy areas, connect data at system level, advise senior stakeholders, improve data quality and collection, and help build analytical capability across Schools Digital?

This is a **G7 leadership analyst** role. They are not just buying someone who can write SQL and build dashboards. They want someone who can set analytical direction, lead complex projects, influence policy/service decisions, and raise analytical standards across teams.

## Most Important Sift Point

The advert says that if they receive a high volume of applications, they may sift on the **first three essential criteria only**. That means the personal statement must front-load these:

1. **Analysis, synthesis and modelling**
2. **Data visualisation and communication**
3. **Technical knowledge**

These three should dominate the first half of the statement.

## Essential Criteria

| Priority | Criterion | What the panel needs to see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Very high** | **Analysis, synthesis and modelling** | Leading and prioritising analysis across related services or policy teams; high-quality insight that drives real decisions; quality-assuring analytical techniques; designing analytical data models. |
| **Very high** | **Data visualisation and communication** | Translating complex data into clear outputs for senior, technical and non-technical audiences; setting direction for analysis; reviewing team outputs and visualisation standards. |
| **Very high** | **Technical knowledge** | Strong SQL; extraction and transformation; reports and dashboards; choosing appropriate tools and advising other analysts on methods. |
| **High** | **Data quality assurance and standards** | Verifying, validating, cleaning data and analysis; improving collection and validation processes; maintaining data quality across teams; ethical standards. |
| **High** | **Delivering impact through data** | Scalable data products and insight outputs aligned to organisational priorities; defining business value; identifying opportunities to maximise data use across programmes. |

## Hidden Buying Criteria

The panel is likely asking:

- Can this person operate at **G7 level**, not just analyst level?
- Can they **lead across multiple teams** rather than one isolated dataset?
- Can they advise **senior stakeholders** with confidence?
- Can they connect analysis to **policy and service impact**?
- Can they improve **data quality, collection and validation** across teams?
- Can they build **reusable/scalable data products**?
- Can they coach and raise the standard of other analysts?
- Can they work in **digital, agile, multidisciplinary delivery**?

## Best-Fit Statement Posture Later

The application should not position Eric as "a strong analyst looking for a bigger role." It should position him as:

> A senior data analyst who has led complex analytical work, built production-shaped data products, improved data quality and validation, translated technical outputs for decision-makers, mentored others, and can set analytical direction across digital services and policy problems.

The biggest thing to prove later is **G7 maturity**: leadership, direction-setting, analytical standards, stakeholder influence, and impact across a programme - not just technical competence.

## Entry 3 - Evidence Sufficiency and G7 Framing Gap

Eric's current examples can support this application, but they do not yet fully suffice for a Grade 7 DfE statement as currently framed.

The evidence base is strong enough to build a credible application, but the framing needs to move up a level. This is not a Band 6 reporting role. DfE is asking for someone who can lead analysis across services/policy areas, set analytical direction, advise senior stakeholders, improve data quality across teams, build scalable data products, and develop other analysts. The advert also warns that if applications are high volume, they may sift on the first three essential criteria only: analysis/synthesis/modelling, visualisation/communication, and technical knowledge.

### Verdict by Evidence Source

| Evidence source | Does it suffice? | How it should be used |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fraud decisioning platform | Strong, but needs senior framing | Best evidence for analytical data models, scalable data products, data quality, dashboards, end-to-end delivery and impact. Must be framed as "designed analytical approach and product," not just "built platform." |
| South Western Data Analyst role | Strong for quality/communication, weaker for G7 leadership | Best evidence for ambiguous multi-source data, stakeholder communication, data quality, decision-ready reporting. Use as supporting evidence, not the main identity. |
| MSc thesis | Useful but not enough alone | Supports modelling, analytical rigour, evaluation and limitations. Keep brief. Do not over-focus on deep learning. |
| Public-data project | Useful bridge to policy context | Good for policy-aware analysis, public datasets, time-series trends and cautious interpretation. This is more DfE-relevant than it first appears. |
| BigQuery / Dataform / Looker / Power BI | Potentially very strong | If Eric has real examples here, this should be foregrounded because GCP tools are a desirable criterion and SQL/cloud analytics are central. |
| Mentoring / training evidence | Moderate | Good for coaching, but not line management. Needs to be framed as developing others' analytical capability, reviewing outputs, documenting standards, and helping people use better methods. |
| Five Guys / Swing Dance | Low for this role | Use only if needed for people leadership or inclusion. It should not appear in the 750-word statement unless absolutely necessary. |

### Criteria-by-Criteria Judgement

#### 1. Analysis, Synthesis and Modelling - Partly Sufficient, Needs Upgrade in Framing

Eric's platform and MSc can cover modelling and analytical design. The platform has enough substance: features, scoring, decision outputs, evaluation, false positives, threshold analysis, monitoring and data-quality checks.

The weak part is the phrase "lead and prioritise data analysis across several related service or policy teams." Eric's examples are not naturally multi-service/policy-team examples. To make this work, frame the platform as analysis across related service components: ingestion, decisioning, case management, monitoring and evaluation. For DfE, the equivalent argument is that he can connect data across a user journey and design analysis that supports multiple decision points.

Needs sharpening: one concise story showing how Eric defined the analytical question, designed the model/data structure, prioritised what mattered, and turned it into a decision-ready output.

#### 2. Data Visualisation and Communication - Sufficient, If We Use the Right Examples

South Western and the platform are strong here. South Western gives non-technical communication: turning "completed but not ready" into management action. The platform gives dashboards: status, reason codes, validation failures, score distributions and threshold effects.

The G7 gap is setting standards and reviewing outputs. Phrase this as: Eric designed dashboards around user questions, separated headline measures from diagnostic views, and documented definitions so outputs could be interpreted consistently.

Needs sharpening: show that he did not just make visuals; he set a standard for what a useful dashboard/report should contain: definition, source, data-quality flag, interpretation and action.

#### 3. Technical Knowledge - Strong If BigQuery/Dataform/Looker/Power BI Are Real and Used

Eric can meet SQL and extraction/transformation through platform, South Western and BigQuery/Dataform if included. This is where the statement should be concrete: SQL transformations, BigQuery datasets, Dataform models, Looker/Power BI dashboards, validation checks, curated tables.

Do not hide the actual tools. For this role, named tools help.

Needs sharpening: state actual SQL/cloud analytics outputs, not just "technical skills."

#### 4. Data Quality Assurance and Standards - Very Strong

This is one of Eric's best areas. South Western gives missing fields, duplicates, failed joins, inconsistent statuses, true zero versus missing submission, and reconciliation. The platform adds validation rules, lineage, monitoring, drift checks and data-quality flags.

This can score highly.

#### 5. Delivering Impact Through Data - Sufficient, But Must Be Made Outcome-Led

The platform reduced manual review burden, prioritised suspicious events and made decisions explainable. South Western reports moved managers from raw status lists to action lists. Public-data project turned large public datasets into policy-aware interpretation.

The DfE statement should say: Eric's work made data usable for decisions, not merely technically correct.

### Biggest Weaknesses to Solve Before Writing

The main missing piece is not technical ability. It is G7 posture.

Right now, Eric's evidence says:

> I can analyse, build, validate and communicate.

For DfE Grade 7, it must say:

> I can set direction for analysis, decide what matters, design reusable data products, raise quality standards, influence stakeholders, and help other analysts do better work.

That means the statement needs stronger examples around:

- setting analytical direction;
- prioritising analysis across connected workstreams;
- reviewing or guiding others' analytical outputs;
- defining standards for dashboards/data products;
- advising senior stakeholders, not just colleagues;
- linking analysis to organisational priorities;
- using BigQuery/Dataform/Looker/Power BI concretely.

### Honest Answer

Eric's current examples are enough to attempt this role, but not enough to max out the application unless they are reframed and enriched around G7 leadership.

The best version should be built around:

1. Platform as the main end-to-end scalable data product and analytical leadership example.
2. BigQuery/Dataform/Looker/Power BI/SQL as the technical proof.
3. South Western as the data-quality and stakeholder-communication proof.
4. Public-data project as the policy-context proof.
5. Mentoring/documentation/training as the analyst-development proof.

The next step should be a short targeted interview focused only on the G7 gaps: analytical direction, senior stakeholder influence, reviewing outputs, setting dashboard/data standards, and BigQuery/Dataform/Looker examples.

## Entry 5 - 750-Word Statement Structure

For this DfE Senior Data Analyst, G7 statement, the structure must be built around the 750-word limit and the warning that, if applications are high volume, they may sift on the first three essential criteria only: analysis, synthesis and modelling; data visualisation and communication; technical knowledge. Those three must dominate the opening half.

### Core Argument

The statement should argue:

> I can lead analytical work from question to decision: define the analytical approach, design trusted datasets and data models, build clear dashboards and insight products, quality-assure analysis, influence stakeholders, and help other analysts work to stronger standards.

This is a G7 statement, so the tone should not be "I can analyse data." It should be:

> I can set analytical direction, improve how teams use data, and turn messy service data into decisions that support policy and digital-service outcomes.

### Recommended Structure: 6 Paragraphs, Around 730-750 Words

| Paragraph | Target words | Argument | Evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Opening fit | 80-90 | I understand the role: leading analysis across digital services and policy areas, using data to improve decisions and outcomes. | Platform, operational reporting, cloud analytics, mentoring. |
| 2. Analysis, synthesis and modelling | 150-170 | I can lead complex analysis by shaping the question, designing the analytical model and connecting data to decisions. | Fraud decisioning platform: ingestion, validation, features, decisions, cases, monitoring, evaluation. |
| 3. Data visualisation and communication | 120-140 | I can turn complex data into clear outputs for technical and non-technical users, with standards for interpretation. | Dashboard layers: headline metrics, diagnostic views, quality flags, reason codes, threshold effects. |
| 4. Technical knowledge | 120-140 | I can use SQL and analytics tools to extract, transform and model data into trusted datasets and dashboards. | BigQuery, Dataform, Looker, Power BI, SQL, Python, curated tables, validation checks. |
| 5. Data quality assurance and standards | 120-140 | I can verify, validate and improve data quality, not just clean data after problems appear. | South Western: row counts, duplicates, missing fields, failed joins, true zero versus missing submission, status reconciliation. |
| 6. Impact, leadership and analyst development | 130-150 | I can define the value of analytical work, influence users, mentor others and build reusable products aligned to organisational priorities. | South Western influence, trainee support, platform documentation, public-data policy project. |

### What Each Paragraph Must Do

#### Paragraph 1 - Opening Fit

This should immediately sound like DfE:

> data across services, user journeys, senior stakeholders, digital services, policy priorities, quality standards, and real-world impact.

Do not open with South Western. Open with the broader G7 capability.

#### Paragraph 2 - Analysis, Synthesis and Modelling

This is the highest-value paragraph. Use the platform as the main story, but frame it as analytical direction, not "I built a fraud system."

The argument:

> I moved from a model-only idea to an analytical product that connected events, validation, features, decisions, cases, monitoring and evaluation.

This proves modelling, synthesis, prioritisation and service-journey thinking.

#### Paragraph 3 - Visualisation and Communication

This should show that dashboards were designed around user decisions.

The argument:

> I designed visual outputs so users could see what happened, why it happened, whether the data was reliable, and what action was needed.

This hits senior stakeholders, technical and non-technical communication, and setting standards.

#### Paragraph 4 - Technical Knowledge

This must name the tools clearly, because DfE values SQL and the desirable criteria mention Google Cloud Platform tools.

Use actual tools:

> BigQuery, Dataform, Looker, Power BI, SQL and Python.

The argument is not tool-listing. It is:

> I used these tools to build curated datasets, transformation logic, dashboard sources, and validation checks.

#### Paragraph 5 - Data Quality Assurance

South Western belongs here.

The argument:

> I can work with ambiguous, conflicting data and create a trusted reporting position by checking definitions, completeness, joins, duplicates, missing records and unexpected movement.

This also supports Managing a Quality Service.

#### Paragraph 6 - Impact, Leadership and Development

This paragraph closes the G7 case.

It should combine:

- business value;
- stakeholder influence;
- mentoring;
- scalable data products;
- standards;
- ethical/responsible interpretation.

The closing should feel like:

> I would bring technical depth, analytical leadership and public-service judgement to Schools Digital.

### What to Avoid

- Do not spend too much space on the MSc thesis.
- Do not use Five Guys unless absolutely needed for leadership/training, and even then only one phrase.
- Do not make the fraud domain the focus; make the analytical product and decision journey the focus.
- Do not include institution names or employer names, because this is name-blind.
- Do not leave the first three essential criteria until later; they must appear before the midpoint.
