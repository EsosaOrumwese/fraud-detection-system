# Department for Education - Senior Data Analyst (G7) Supporting Statement

This is a first 750-word statement draft. It is name-blind, front-loads the first three essential criteria, and keeps the focus on G7-level analytical leadership rather than simply "I can analyse data." The advert says the statement should be a maximum of 750 words and may be sifted on the first three essential criteria if application volume is high.

## Statement of Suitability Draft

I am applying for this Grade 7 Senior Data Analyst role because my experience is centred on leading analytical work from unclear data questions through to trusted datasets, visual outputs, quality-assured insight and decision support. I have built data products, designed analytical models, worked with SQL and cloud analytics tools, created dashboards, improved data quality controls and supported others to understand and use analysis. I am interested in this role because it connects digital services, policy priorities and user-centred delivery, where strong analysis can help teams decide where services, data collection and policy interventions need to improve.

A strong example of my analytical leadership is my independent fraud and transaction decisioning platform. The first version could score sample events, but I recognised that a model score alone would not solve the decision problem. I redesigned the work as an analytical product covering ingestion, validation, feature creation, rule and model decisioning, case creation, monitoring and offline evaluation. I prioritised the parts that made the output usable: time-safe feature definitions, decision reason codes, case status, validation flags, threshold analysis and performance monitoring. This meant the analysis answered what was flagged, why it was flagged, whether the data was reliable, what action was needed and how the process should be evaluated over time.

I have applied the same approach to data visualisation and communication. For the platform, I designed dashboard outputs around user questions rather than chart volume. The headline view showed event volumes, flagged cases, case status and validation failures. Diagnostic views showed reason codes, rule triggers, score distributions, threshold effects, false positives, false negatives and data-quality issues. This helped technical users investigate behaviour while allowing non-technical users to understand what needed attention and what the limits of the data were. In an operational analyst role, I also translated ambiguous source records into action-led exception views, explaining which items were complete, blocked, awaiting documentation or requiring follow-up.

Technically, I have strong SQL and data-transformation experience across workplace reporting, cloud analytics and project work. I have used BigQuery and Dataform to structure SQL transformation logic into reusable analytical models, separating raw data, cleaned data and reporting-ready outputs so dashboards are not built from one-off queries. I have used Looker and Power BI to present trends, exceptions, quality indicators and drill-down views. I have also used Python for preprocessing, modelling and evaluation, and Excel for validation packs, formulas, pivot tables, lookups, variance checks and management summaries. My focus is to make the dataset trusted before it becomes a dashboard: clear definitions, tested transformations, documented assumptions and repeatable checks.

Data quality assurance is one of my strongest areas. In an operational Data Analyst role, I worked with complex records from several sources, including enterprise extracts, transformation outputs, spreadsheets, local databases, safety records, man-hour sheets, equipment movement notes and close-out files. These sources did not always agree. I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, failed joins, inconsistent statuses, unexpected movements and whether a blank value represented a true zero or a missing submission. I improved reporting categories so a broad "completed" status was separated into field-completed, awaiting documentation, awaiting equipment confirmation and ready for close-out. This changed the conversation from disagreement about data to specific actions and clearer management decisions.

I have delivered impact by turning analysis into reusable products and better decisions. The platform reduced manual inspection by prioritising suspicious events, explaining decisions through reason codes and monitoring whether thresholds created too much review work. In public-data analysis, I combined over one thousand daily files into a multi-million-row dataset, selected appropriate metrics, visualised time-series trends and interpreted patterns alongside policy context without overstating causation. In operational reporting, exception views helped managers see blockers and follow-up actions. Across these examples, I define the value of analysis as helping users decide what to do differently, not simply producing technically correct outputs.

I have also supported analytical capability in others. In a workplace setting, I helped trainees understand reporting workbooks, validation checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation, setting small checking tasks and reviewing their outputs. Through documentation, dashboard definitions and reusable SQL models, I have made analytical work easier for others to understand, repeat and challenge. I would bring to Schools Digital a combination of technical depth, quality assurance, stakeholder communication and analytical leadership: setting clear analytical direction, building trusted data products, improving collection and validation processes, and helping teams use evidence to improve digital services and policy outcomes for children, young people and teachers.
