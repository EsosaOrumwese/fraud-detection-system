# Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust - Senior Information Analyst Supporting Statement

## Supporting Statement

I am applying for the Senior Information Analyst role because my experience aligns strongly with producing accurate BI outputs from complex data: SQL extraction, Excel and Power BI reporting, data-quality checks, trend analysis, exception reporting and clear communication with service users. I understand this post supports Women's and Children's services by providing reliable information to clinicians, senior managers and external stakeholders so they can monitor activity, performance, patient flow and service quality. I would bring strong technical BI capability, careful data-quality discipline and experience turning operational records into information that supports decisions.

I meet the degree-level requirement through a First Class BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter. My postgraduate study strengthened my SQL, Python, statistics, data modelling, visualisation, model evaluation and responsible interpretation skills. My engineering background also developed my numerical reasoning, structured problem-solving and ability to work carefully with complex technical information.

My strongest workplace evidence comes from South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I worked in field engineering and operations analytics support. The domain was different from NHS BI, but the reporting discipline was directly relevant: complex operational data, multiple sources, conflicting records, deadlines, data-quality checks, management summaries and communication with users. I worked with Oracle data extracts, SAS outputs, Excel trackers, Access tables, equipment movement notes, service records, QHSE submissions, man-hour records and operational exceptions. These sources did not always agree, so my role was to check, reconcile and turn them into reliable management information.

I have strong SQL, Excel and BI reporting experience. I have used SQL to query, join, filter, transform and validate data, creating report-ready outputs and checks for inconsistencies. In Excel, I have built structured workbooks using formulas, pivot tables, lookups, conditional formatting, validation checks, charts, variance views and management summaries. I have used Access and relational database concepts to organise structured local datasets where spreadsheets alone were not robust enough. I have also used Power BI to present trends, exceptions, data-quality indicators and drill-down views. In independent analytics work, I created data layers, validation rules and dashboard measures, with source fields mapped to each measure so figures could be checked back to the underlying data.

Data quality and accuracy are central to how I work. At South Western, a job could appear complete in one record while still having missing equipment confirmation, incomplete close-out details, unclear delay descriptions or unresolved maintenance issues. If those issues were not identified, a report could give managers a false view of readiness. I separated raw records, cleaned datasets, exception views and final reporting outputs so users could see what was genuinely complete, what was blocked and what required action. I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, failed joins, inconsistent statuses, unclear service categories, unexpected movements between reporting periods and differences between source extracts and final outputs. I also learned not to treat missing submissions as true zero values, because incomplete data could make performance look cleaner than it really was.

I can explain trends, themes and exceptions in data. In operational reporting, I analysed job readiness, delay trends, equipment availability, QHSE completeness, inventory exceptions and maintenance follow-up using rates, percentages, variance checks, trend views, standard deviation, Pareto breakdowns and completeness checks. In my MSc and independent platform work, I also worked with time-series patterns, model outputs, score distributions, threshold effects and validation failures. This gives me a strong base for analysing activity, explaining variation, identifying exceptions, supporting activity forecasting and monitoring target compliance under local NHS definitions.

I understand that NHS BI reporting depends on using agreed definitions, data items, datasets, coding rules and pathway logic consistently. I have developed a working understanding of NHS Data Dictionary principles, including how data items, datasets, clinical terminology, coding rules, inclusion rules, exclusions, reporting periods and pathway events affect whether a report is valid. I would not assume that a familiar-looking metric means the same thing locally without checking the Trust's source systems, dataset definitions, pathway rules and reporting guidance. For Women's and Children's services, I would first understand the relevant activity events, clinical context, local data flows and the decision the report is meant to support.

I can present information clearly to different audiences and challenge assumptions constructively. At South Western, I used written summaries, exception views, dashboard outputs and verbal explanations to show what the data said, what was uncertain and what action was needed. I adapted the level of detail depending on whether I was speaking to field teams, inventory colleagues, commercial colleagues or managers. When a job was described as "complete," I challenged whether that meant field-completed, documentation complete, equipment returned, maintenance cleared or ready for close-out. That moved the conversation from a vague status label to the specific record or action needed. I would use the same evidence-led approach with NHS reporting requests: clarify the question, check the definition, identify the best source, test whether the data supports the request, and explain any limitation before the figure is used.

I can work independently, prioritise deadlines and support others. In operational reporting, I balanced scheduled reports with ad-hoc clarifications, unresolved data-quality issues and urgent questions. I prioritised checks that most affected output reliability, separated confirmed information from items requiring clarification, and kept unresolved issues visible through exception lists and follow-up notes. I understand the importance of routine information flows and would treat recurring outputs, ad-hoc requests and external reporting deadlines as controlled reporting processes requiring clear definitions, validation and sign-off. I also supported NYSC students and industrial training students by explaining reporting workbooks, Excel checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation, setting small checking tasks and reviewing outputs before figures were used.

I understand the importance of confidentiality, data protection and information governance in a healthcare BI role. My working approach is that data should be used for a clear purpose, shared only at the level required, checked for accuracy and handled in line with policy. I would avoid sharing identifiable or sensitive data beyond the agreed purpose, escalate concerns where data quality or disclosure risk could affect safe reporting, and follow Trust and NHS protocols carefully.

I would bring to Gloucestershire Hospitals a strong combination of SQL, Excel, Power BI, analytical judgement, data-quality discipline and people-focused communication. I am motivated by the Trust's commitment to "Best Care for Everyone" and by the opportunity to help Women's and Children's services use accurate information to improve patient experience, service quality, patient flow and operational decisions. I am also willing to undertake weekend work where required by the service. I would aim to contribute through reliable SQL outputs, clear dashboards, robust validation checks, respectful stakeholder challenge and continual improvement.
