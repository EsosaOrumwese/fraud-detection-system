# East Sussex Healthcare NHS Trust - Information Analyst Supporting Statement

## Revised Supporting Statement

I am applying for the Information Analyst role because my experience is strongly aligned with preparing, validating, analysing and presenting complex data to support operational decision-making. I understand that this post sits within Information Management and Performance, with a focus on producing accurate, timely and actionable intelligence for managers, clinicians and service users, while also supporting dashboards, self-service reporting, data quality, ad-hoc analysis and service improvement. My background combines operational data analysis, advanced Excel and SQL work, relational database use, dashboard development, data-quality assurance, postgraduate data science training and communication with non-technical users.

My education and training have given me a strong technical foundation for the role. I have strong Maths and English qualifications, a BEng Mechanical Engineering degree from Covenant University, and an MSc Data Science with Artificial Intelligence from the University of Exeter. My academic and professional background has required numerical reasoning, written communication, technical analysis, problem-solving and the ability to explain complex information clearly. My MSc also strengthened my skills in data preprocessing, statistical modelling, Python, SQL, visualisation, machine learning, model evaluation and responsible interpretation of results.

My strongest workplace evidence comes from my Data Analyst role at South Western Technologies & Oilfield Services Ltd, where I supported reporting across service activity, equipment movement, QHSE records, man-hours, close-out documentation and management information. The role involved preparing recurring and ad-hoc reports from multiple operational sources, including Oracle extracts, SAS transformation outputs, Excel trackers, Access tables, equipment movement notes, service records and QHSE submissions. The data was not always clean or consistent, so my work involved turning complex operational records into reliable information that managers and colleagues could use to understand performance, delays, documentation gaps and required actions.

A typical issue was that a job could be marked as completed in one source but still be missing a signed service report, equipment return confirmation, man-hour record or close-out status. If this was reported simply as "completed", it could create a misleading view of readiness. I therefore separated raw extracts, cleaned datasets, exception views and final reporting outputs, creating categories such as active, delayed, field-completed, awaiting documentation, awaiting equipment confirmation and ready for close-out. This made reporting more accurate and helped users understand not only what the figures said, but what needed to happen next.

I have strong experience with SQL, Microsoft SQL Server, relational databases and Excel. I have used Microsoft SQL Server and SQL to query, join, filter, transform and validate data, working with relational structures, keys, tables, joins, validation queries and report-ready outputs. I have used Access to organise structured local datasets, create tables and queries, and manage recurring reporting inputs where a spreadsheet alone was not robust enough. In Excel, I have built structured workbooks using formulas, pivot tables, lookups, conditional formatting, validation checks, charts, percentages, variances, trend views and management summaries. I have also used Power BI to present status views, exceptions, data-quality indicators and drill-down dashboards for users.

Data quality and validation are central to how I work. At South Western, I checked row counts, duplicate records, missing fields, failed joins, inconsistent statuses, unclear service categories, unexpected movements between reporting periods and differences between source extracts and final reports. In QHSE reporting, I learned not to treat missing submissions as true zero values. A low recorded incident figure might reflect low risk, but it might also reflect incomplete reporting. I used rates, percentage changes, variances, trend views, graphs and completeness checks to make sure figures were interpreted safely before they were shared.

My wider applied data work has also strengthened my ability to work with large datasets, dashboards and advanced analytical methods. During postgraduate and independent analytics work, I built dashboards and data products involving ingestion, validation, transformation, monitoring, evaluation and predictive modelling concepts. I also completed a public-data project combining over one thousand daily files into a multi-million-row dataset, using Python and pandas to clean, combine, analyse and visualise time-series patterns. I am comfortable improving reporting outputs iteratively by defining the requirement, building the report, validating the figures, documenting assumptions, releasing the output and updating it when user needs or data definitions change.

I understand that NHS information analysis requires careful handling of patient-level information, national standards, clinical datasets, care pathways and confidentiality. Although my direct employment background is outside the NHS, I have developed working knowledge of NHS information concepts, including data standards, Data Dictionary definitions, clinical datasets, patient pathways, activity reporting, performance monitoring and accurate event coding. I would approach East Sussex Healthcare's local systems carefully by learning the Trust's definitions, reporting rules, data flows, validation processes and information governance requirements before making assumptions about any field or metric.

I also understand the importance of the Data Protection Act 2018, confidentiality and secure handling of patient-level information. My working approach is that data should be used only for a clear purpose, shared only at the level required, checked for accuracy, and handled in line with policy and legal obligations. In an NHS setting, I would protect identifiable information, check disclosure risk, validate figures and escalate concerns where data quality or confidentiality could affect safe reporting.

Communication is one of my strongest transferable areas. At South Western, I worked with operations, equipment, QHSE, admin, commercial colleagues and managers, adapting my communication for users with different levels of technical understanding. I used written summaries, exception views, dashboard outputs and verbal explanations to show what the data said, what was uncertain and what action was needed. I also supported trainees by explaining reporting workbooks, Excel checks, operational definitions and exception interpretation, setting small checking tasks and reviewing their outputs before figures were used.

I am comfortable working independently, prioritising under pressure and concentrating for long periods on complex data tasks. In operational reporting, I balanced recurring reports with ad-hoc clarifications, unresolved data-quality issues and deadlines. I prioritised the checks that most affected the reliability of the final output, separated confirmed information from items requiring clarification, and kept unresolved issues visible through exception lists and follow-up notes. I would bring the same structured approach to routine reports, FOI or ad-hoc enquiries, dashboard maintenance and service-improvement analysis.

I would bring to East Sussex Healthcare NHS Trust strong SQL and Excel capability, relational database understanding, dashboard development, data-quality discipline and service-focused communication. I would work in line with the Trust values of Working Together, Respect and Compassion, Engagement and Involvement, and Improvement and Development by collaborating constructively, communicating respectfully, helping users engage with information, and improving the quality, clarity and usefulness of reporting outputs.
