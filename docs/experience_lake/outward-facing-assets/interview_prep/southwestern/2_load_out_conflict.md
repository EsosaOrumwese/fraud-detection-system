# Title: Holding the load-out after a maintenance-record conflict at Warri base

Use this as the **overall idea** for Territory 2:

## Story concept

**Title:** Holding the load-out after a maintenance-record conflict at Warri base

**Context / scene**

Eric is at **SouthWestern’s Warri base**, preparing for a **swamp well-intervention/completion support job at Diebu Creek Field, OML 32**.

The crew is meant to leave that afternoon with the equipment package: hydraulic power unit, hose bundle, tong package, torque gauge, lifting accessories, hand tools, and completion/tubular-running support items.

The truck is already booked. Logistics wants to load and leave. The senior operator says the equipment has been used before and is physically available in the yard. The field supervisor wants no delay because the client is expecting the crew at site.

Eric’s assigned task is to complete the **load-out readiness check**: match the equipment list against the physical equipment, maintenance status, asset register/ERP record, and job dispatch notes.

## Core conflict

During the check, Eric finds that one major item — likely the **hydraulic power unit or hose bundle** — is physically present in the yard but still shows as **pending post-job inspection / maintenance close-out** from the previous job.

The senior operator disagrees with holding the load-out. From his view, the equipment is there, the crew knows it, and the job should move.

Eric’s position is difficult because he is younger, less experienced in the field, and looks like the person delaying the job because of “paperwork.”

## Why this fits Territory 2

This story gives us:

* **Conflict in a team:** field operations wants to dispatch; maintenance/records show the package is not fully cleared.
* **Difficult person:** senior operator or logistics lead pushes back hard because they feel Eric is slowing the job.
* **Disagreement with your approach:** they want to load based on physical availability; Eric wants maintenance clearance before release.
* **Difficult feedback:** Eric has to tell experienced people the package should not be marked ready yet.
* **Misunderstood:** they see Eric as the junior data/records person blocking field work.
* **Regaining trust:** Eric goes beyond the ERP screen, checks the asset tag, asks maintenance what exactly is pending, finds a practical way to clear or swap the item, and gets the crew moving.
* **Unpopular decision:** Eric supports holding the load-out briefly until the equipment status is resolved.

## Why this is stronger than another rig-floor story

It uses a different facet of the role: **ERP / asset / inventory / maintenance analysis**.

The conflict is human and operational. People are under dispatch pressure. Senior people disagree. Eric has to speak carefully, because being correct is not enough if he sounds like he is lecturing experienced operators.

The story question becomes:

**How did Eric hold a standard without losing the trust of the field crew?**
