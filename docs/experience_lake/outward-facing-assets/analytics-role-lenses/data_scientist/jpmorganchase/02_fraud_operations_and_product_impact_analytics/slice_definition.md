## 1. Requirement Being Targeted

The requirement being targeted here is the `JPMorganChase` `Data Scientist - Fraud Strategic Analytics Associate` expectation around:
- conducting analytics to support the fraud product
- conducting analytics to support fraud operations
- conducting analytics to support financial-crime activity
- protecting the financial interests of customers and the bank through analytics
- translating fraud strategy into operational impact
- supporting fraud operations to improve efficiency
- supporting fraud operations to improve decision accuracy

This maps directly to the employer evidence that the candidate should be able to sound like someone who:
- uses analytics to change how fraud teams work, not only how fraud is described
- can show which fraud strategies or controls materially change operational yield or workload
- can support product and operations teams with bounded decision-use evidence
- can frame the effect of a strategy change in terms of efficiency, prioritisation, and decision quality

This note exists because that requirement is easy to flatten into the vague statement:
`good at fraud analytics for operations`

That is not the real burden.

The employer is asking for something sharper:
- fraud-product analytics
- fraud-operations support
- financial-crime-support analytics
- translation of strategy into operational impact
- decision-quality and efficiency improvement

## 2. The Analogue On The Current Platform

The current platform does not contain a literal `JPMorganChase` fraud-operations team queue, product squad workflow, or financial-crime operating model, so the slice has to use an honest analogue.

The closest truthful analogue is:
- one governed operational-impact base built from the same fraud-decision lane
- one prioritisation or workload-effect surface that shows how the selected posture changes downstream use
- one decision-quality or yield surface that shows how operations or product would benefit
- one operational-impact note that explains what becomes more efficient or more accurate under the preferred posture

So for this slice:
- `conduct analytics to support fraud product` maps to `one bounded product-support reading over the same governed fraud lane`
- `conduct analytics to support fraud operations` maps to `one bounded prioritisation and workload-impact surface`
- `translate fraud strategy into operational impact` maps to `one explicit before-versus-after operational reading`
- `improve efficiency` maps to `one selection-burden or workload-improvement surface`
- `improve decision accuracy` maps to `one positive-yield or decision-quality surface`

The claim must therefore remain:
- fraud-operations shaped
- product-support shaped
- operational-impact shaped
- efficiency shaped
- decision-quality shaped
- careful not to imply ownership of a live fraud-operations function, full digital-bank product strategy, or full financial-crime programme where this slice only proves one bounded operational-impact object

Default first-pass posture:
- operational-impact base first
- prioritisation or workload surface second
- decision-quality surface third
- product-and-operations reading fourth
- stronger queue, workflow, or product-delivery rhetoric only if later slices genuinely support it
